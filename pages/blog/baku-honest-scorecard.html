Baku is one of the most underrated destinations Indian travellers can reach right now — a medieval Islamic Old City, Soviet brutalist architecture, futuristic glass towers, and Caspian Sea sunsets, all in a country that's genuinely affordable compared to Europe. The Flame Towers at night look like something out of a sci-fi film. The food is a hybrid of Turkish, Persian, and Russian influences. And almost nobody from India goes there, which means you'll have the good stuff largely to yourself.

We've rated Baku across 8 categories — honestly, not as a tourism board would. Here's our full verdict, what you should know before you go, and how to do it without wasting money.

Overall Tripofreak Verdict

Baku punches well above its weight for Indian travellers. High safety, excellent value, jaw-dropping architecture, and a genuine sense of discovery — the kind of trip you'll talk about. The food gap for vegetarians is real, but solvable. If you've done Thailand, Bali, and Singapore — Baku is exactly where to go next.

What Works

  • Flame Towers at night — genuinely stunning
  • Old City (Icherisheher) — a UNESCO-listed gem
  • Very safe for solo travellers and families
  • Much cheaper than Dubai for a similar "wow" factor
  • Almost no Indian tourist crowds
  • Caspian Sea sunsets from the Boulevard
  • Easy e-Visa online, no embassy hassle
  • Excellent 4-star hotels at Bangkok prices
  • Day trips: Gobustan, Mud Volcanoes, Ateshgah Fire Temple

What Doesn't

  • Very limited pure vegetarian restaurant options
  • Language barrier — Azerbaijani, some Russian; English patchy outside tourist zones
  • Taxi drivers often try to overcharge tourists
  • Most attractions seen in 4–5 days — not worth 2 weeks
  • Alcohol is widely available but public intoxication creates occasional discomfort
  • Shopping is limited — no Bangkok-level markets
  • Currency exchange not always straightforward

Go to Baku if you...

  • Want something genuinely different from your usual Asia circuit
  • Love architecture, history, and photography
  • Eat non-veg and enjoy trying new cuisines
  • Want Dubai-level "wow" at half the cost
  • Are a couple, solo traveller, or small group of friends
  • Have already done SEA and want something new

Think twice if you...

  • Are strictly vegetarian or vegan — it'll be a daily struggle
  • Are travelling with young kids seeking theme parks and beaches
  • Want 10+ days of sightseeing in one city
  • Are uncomfortable with limited English-speaking locals
  • Want shopping as a primary activity

Visa for Indians — Complete Guide

Indians need a visa for Azerbaijan. The good news: it's one of the easiest visa processes for Indian travellers — fully online, no embassy visit, no interview, no biometrics.

  • 1
    Go to evisa.gov.az

    The official ASAN Visa portal. Apply 5–7 days before travel to be safe, though approval usually comes in 3 business days.

  • 2
    Fill in the application

    You'll need your passport (valid 6+ months beyond travel), a passport photo, accommodation details, and travel dates. The process takes about 15 minutes.

  • 3
    Pay the fee — USD 26 (~₹2,180)

    Pay by credit/debit card. Indian cards generally work fine on this portal. Keep the payment receipt.

  • 4
    Download your e-Visa PDF

    You get a PDF visa. Print it or keep it on your phone. Show at Baku Heydar Aliyev Airport immigration — the officer will stamp your passport.

Visa Validity

30 days single entry from the date of issue. Arrive within 90 days of issue date.

Visa on Arrival

Available at Baku airport for USD 30 but queues can be 45–60 minutes. Apply online instead.

Processing Time

Typically 3 business days. Apply at least 5 days before departure.

Passport Required

Valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended stay. Expired/damaged passports refused.

Pro Tip — Visa

Screenshot or download your e-Visa to your phone before travel. Airport Wi-Fi can be slow and you don't want to fumble at immigration. Also keep a printed copy as backup — Baku immigration occasionally wants paper.

Getting There — Flights from India

No direct flights from India to Baku currently. You'll connect via Dubai, Istanbul, or Sharjah. Flight times: 6–9 hours total with layover. The cheapest routes are via Flydubai (Dubai connection) or Air Arabia (Sharjah connection). Turkish Airlines via Istanbul is the most comfortable but more expensive.

Getting Around Baku

Baku is compact and relatively easy to navigate. The city centre, Old City, and most attractions are walkable or a short metro ride apart.

Metro

Clean, fast, cheap. Fare: 0.30 AZN (~₹15) per ride. Buy a BakıKart (~₹60 deposit). Covers main tourist areas but not all neighbourhoods. Announcements in Azerbaijani — memorise your stop or use Google Maps offline.

Taxi (Bolt / Uber)

Bolt is the dominant app in Baku — cheap, reliable, English-language app. Fares: ₹100–250 for most in-city rides. Never take unmetered street taxis — tourist prices are 3–5× the app rate.

Walking

The Old City, Boulevard, and Fountain Square area are best explored on foot. The Old City streets are stone-paved and very walkable. Wear comfortable shoes — the cobblestones are uneven.

Day Trips

Gobustan, Mud Volcanoes, and Ateshgah are 30–60 min from Baku. Hire a private driver or join a group tour (₹1,500–3,000/person). No direct public transport to most of these.

Food in Baku — What Indian Travellers Need to Know

Azerbaijani cuisine is genuinely excellent — heavily influenced by Turkish, Persian, and Russian cooking. The signature dishes are plov (saffron rice with lamb), dolma (stuffed grape leaves), kebabs (every variety imaginable), lavangi (stuffed chicken or fish), and düşbərə (tiny lamb dumplings in broth). The bread — tendir bread, fresh from clay ovens — is exceptional.

The issue: Baku is not vegetarian-friendly by default. Most traditional dishes involve meat or meat-based broths. That said, you can manage:

  • Most restaurants have plov without meat (plain rice with saffron and dried fruits — actually delicious)
  • Bread + cheese + herb platters are available everywhere and form a solid veg meal
  • Indian restaurants exist in Baku — Mughal, Taj Mahal Restaurant, and a few others near the Old City
  • Pizza and pasta restaurants are easy to find with veg options
  • Supermarkets (Bravo, Araz) have good ready-to-eat options
For Vegetarians

Plan ahead. You won't starve, but eating out every meal without planning will result in a lot of "bread + tomato salad" dinners. Look up Indian restaurants near your hotel before you go, and always ask "etsiz?" (without meat) at local restaurants.

Where to Eat

Old City restaurants are atmospheric but often tourist-priced. Walk 2 blocks outside the walls and prices drop 40%. The central market (Taza Bazar) area has cheap, authentic teahouses. For a blowout dinner, Şirvanşah Muzey Restoran inside the Old City is worth it — traditional Azerbaijani food in a 15th-century caravanserai setting.

Common Mistakes Indian Travellers Make in Baku

  • Using street taxis instead of Bolt — street drivers at the airport can charge ₹1,500+ for a ₹300 Bolt ride. Always use the app.
  • Not downloading offline maps — Google Maps works but data can be patchy. Download the Baku map offline before leaving your hotel.
  • Exchanging money at the airport — airport rates are poor. Exchange at central banks or use your card at ATMs (use a zero-forex fee card if you have one).
  • Skipping the day trips — Gobustan petroglyphs and Mud Volcanoes are 45 minutes from Baku and unlike anything in the world. Don't skip them to save one day in the city.
  • Visiting only the tourist-zone restaurants — Fountain Square and Old City restaurants are overpriced by local standards. Walk 5 minutes out and eat better for half the price.
  • Assuming everyone speaks English — older locals often speak Russian or Azerbaijani only. Google Translate (Azerbaijani pack downloaded offline) is essential.

Emergency & Practical Information

Ambulance: 103
Fire: 101
General Emergency: 112

Indian Embassy

Embassy of India, Baku
Address: 2 Hasan Aliyev Street
Phone: +994 12 596 0300
Open: Mon–Fri, 9am–5pm

SIM Card

Buy at Baku airport — Azercell or Bakcell tourist SIMs. 10GB for ~AZN 10 (~₹500). Works well across the city; coverage drops in rural areas.

Currency / ATMs

Azerbaijani Manat (AZN). ATMs widely available. Mastercard works better than Visa at local ATMs. Carry some USD as backup for small vendors.

Medical

ADA Clinic and Medline Hospital are the best hospitals for tourists. English-speaking doctors available. Bring travel insurance — medical costs for tourists can be high without it.

Plug Type

Type C and F (European 2-pin round). Standard 220V. Indian plugs don't fit directly — bring a universal adapter. Most hotels have adapters at the front desk.
