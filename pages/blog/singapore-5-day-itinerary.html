Singapore 5-Day Itinerary for Indian Travellers (2026)

5 Days · Day-by-Day Plan Updated: September 2026 Budget: ₹60K–₹1.8L Best for: First-timers & Families

Singapore is one of the easiest international trips for Indian travellers — no culture shock, no language barrier, exceptional food at every price point, and transport that actually runs on time. Five days is the sweet spot: enough to hit the big landmarks, eat your way through hawker centres, and still have breathing room. This is the exact itinerary I'd give a friend heading there for the first time.

Before you go, read what nobody tells you about Singapore — it'll save you money and avoid the tourist traps. Our Singapore honest scorecard (8.1/10) also breaks down exactly where the city delivers and where it disappoints.

The 5-Day Plan at a Glance

Here's the logic behind the order: Day 1 is an easy arrival day to settle in and get oriented. Day 2 is Sentosa while you still have energy. Day 3 is the cultural deep-dive that Indian travellers genuinely love. Day 4 is the big-ticket Marina Bay circuit. Day 5 is the Night Safari — perfect before a late flight, or a slow morning wrap-up before flying home.

Day 1 Arrival · Orchard Road · Clarke Quay
  • Morning
    Land at Changi Airport — the world's best airport

    Changi is an experience in itself. If you haven't already, walk through Jewel Changi (it's connected to T1/T2/T3) — there's a 40m indoor waterfall, a rain vortex, and tonnes of food. Budget 45 minutes here before leaving the airport. Take the MRT from Changi Airport station directly to your hotel — cost ~SGD 1.90 (~₹120).

  • Afternoon
    Check in + Orchard Road walk

    Drop your bags. Then head to Orchard Road — Singapore's main shopping belt. You don't need to buy anything; just walk the stretch from ION Orchard to Lucky Plaza. Lucky Plaza has several Indian and Filipino remittance shops if you need to exchange currency at decent rates. Grab a kaya toast + soft-boiled egg set at Ya Kun Kaya Toast for a true Singaporean snack (~SGD 4, ₹250).

  • Evening
    Clarke Quay for dinner + nightlife

    Take the MRT to Clarke Quay. The riverside is beautiful at night. Dinner at a hawker centre nearby first (Maxwell Food Centre or Lau Pa Sat — budget SGD 6–10 per person), then stroll Clarke Quay for drinks if you want. Warning: the restaurants on the waterfront itself are tourist-priced. Eat at hawker, drink at Clarke Quay if at all.

Budget Day: ₹2,000–3,500 Mid-range Day: ₹5,000–8,000
Day 2 Sentosa Island — Beaches, Universal Studios, or Both
  • Morning
    Head to Sentosa early (before 10am)

    Take the Sentosa Express monorail from VivoCity mall (SGD 4 return, ₹250) or walk across the Sentosa Boardwalk for free. Getting there early matters — Universal Studios queues are manageable before 11am and brutal after noon. If you're skipping Universal, the beaches (Siloso, Palawan, Tanjong) are free and beautiful in the morning light.

  • Mid-Morning
    Universal Studios Singapore (optional, ₹4,500–5,500)

    One-day tickets are SGD 83 (~₹5,200). Worth it if you have kids or love theme parks. Key rides: Transformers the Ride, Battlestar Galactica, Revenge of the Mummy, Jurassic World. Skip: the parade (not worth the wait). If you're skipping Universal, S.E.A. Aquarium (~SGD 41, ₹2,600) is excellent — one of the world's largest, stunning tanks.

  • Afternoon
    Siloso Beach or Palawan Beach

    After rides or aquarium, head to Siloso Beach for a few hours. The water is calm, the beach is clean, and there are food kiosks. Palawan Beach has a suspension bridge to the "Southernmost Point of Continental Asia" — touristy but the walk is nice. Tanjong Beach is the nicest but quieter.

  • Evening
    Wings of Time (optional, SGD 18–28) or head back early

    Wings of Time is an outdoor light-and-water show on Siloso Beach at 7:40pm and 8:40pm. It's genuinely impressive — especially for families. Otherwise head back to VivoCity for dinner at the food court (budget-friendly, great variety including Indian options).

Without Universal: ₹3,000–5,000 With Universal: ₹9,000–12,000/person
Pro Tip — Sentosa

Buy Universal Studios tickets online 2–3 days in advance — they're often cheaper than at the gate and skip the long queue at the ticket counter. Also, bring a small backpack with water and sunscreen. The sun in Singapore is fierce even when it's cloudy.

Day 3 Little India · Chinatown · Arab Street · Hawker Crawl
  • Morning
    Little India — Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple + Tekka Centre

    Start at Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple on Serangoon Road (free entry, remove shoes). Then head to Tekka Centre wet market and hawker — one of the most authentic non-tourist hawker centres in Singapore. Get the roti prata, fish head curry, or banana leaf rice for breakfast. Budget SGD 4–7 (~₹250–440). Little India on a Sunday is peak energy — avoid if you want a quiet morning.

  • Mid-Morning
    Mustafa Centre — the 24-hour Indian department store

    Two floors of everything — electronics, clothes, spices, Indian snacks, gold. Best prices in Singapore for many things. Pick up your snacks and Singapore souvenirs here rather than at tourist shops. Budget 45 minutes. Prices are fixed, no bargaining.

  • Afternoon
    Chinatown — Sri Mariamman Temple + Chinatown Complex

    Walk to Chinatown (MRT: Chinatown station). Visit Sri Mariamman Temple — Singapore's oldest Hindu temple, right in the middle of Chinatown (irony intentional by the British). Then Chinatown Complex has the best char kway teow and bak chor mee. Chinatown Street Market outside has cheap souvenirs — magnets, keychains — if you need to buy gifts.

  • Evening
    Arab Street + Haji Lane

    Walk or MRT to Bugis/Arab Street. The Sultan Mosque at golden hour is stunning — you can enter respectfully (cover up). Haji Lane is a narrow street full of indie boutiques, street art, and cafes. Dinner at Zam Zam restaurant — it's been here since 1908, murtabak is legendary. Budget SGD 10–15 (~₹630–950).

Budget Day: ₹1,800–3,000 Mid-range Day: ₹4,000–7,000
Indian Traveller Note

Little India is the most "home-feeling" part of Singapore — Tamil signage, South Indian restaurants, jasmine garlands, Bollywood music on the streets. If you're travelling with older family members who aren't adventurous eaters, make this a base camp. The Mustafa Centre area has comfortable guesthouses that are a fraction of Marina Bay hotel prices.

Day 4 Marina Bay · Gardens by the Bay · Singapore Flyer
  • Morning
    Marina Bay Sands — free areas + Helix Bridge walk

    You don't need to pay for the MBS infinity pool (hotel guests only — remember this). But the ground-level The Shoppes mall is free, The Sands Expo is free to walk, and the view from the waterfront promenade is the classic Singapore shot. Walk across the Helix Bridge for a different angle. Best photos: 7–8am (golden hour, few crowds) or after dark.

  • Mid-Morning
    Gardens by the Bay — Supertree Grove (free) + Flower Dome (paid)

    The Supertree Grove walkway is free and stunning. If you want to go inside, Cloud Forest (~SGD 28, ₹1,760) is the must-do — a 35m indoor mountain with a misty waterfall and tropical plants. Flower Dome is the world's largest glass greenhouse but less dramatic. Do Cloud Forest and skip Flower Dome if choosing one. OCBC Skyway (the walkway between Supertrees) is SGD 14 (~₹880) — optional.

  • Afternoon
    Merlion Park + Esplanade

    Walk to Merlion Park — yes it's touristy, yes get the photo. Then walk along the river to the Esplanade (the "Durian" building). The rooftop of Esplanade is free and gives beautiful views. The Esplanade has a free outdoor performance stage with live music most evenings. Lunch at Lau Pa Sat (Telok Ayer Market) — open-air hawker in a Victorian cast-iron building (~SGD 6–12, ₹380–760).

  • Evening
    Singapore Flyer (optional) + Garden Rhapsody light show

    Singapore Flyer is the giant observation wheel — SGD 40 (~₹2,500). It's good but not essential if you're budget-conscious. The view from Marina Bay promenade at night is arguably better and free. Garden Rhapsody at Gardens by the Bay happens nightly at 7:45pm and 8:45pm — a free 10-minute light and music show on the Supertrees. Don't miss it.

Budget Day: ₹3,500–5,500 Mid-range Day: ₹7,000–12,000
Day 5 Night Safari · Last Bites · Departure
  • Morning
    Slow morning — pack and last hawker breakfast

    Check-out is usually 11am or 12pm. Have a final breakfast at a hawker centre — kaya toast with soft-boiled eggs and kopi (Singaporean black coffee) is the classic send-off. Newton Food Centre or your hotel-area hawker works fine. Do any last-minute shopping at Mustafa or a duty-free mall.

  • Afternoon
    Store luggage + Bugis area or Kampong Glam

    Most hotels allow you to leave luggage at reception after check-out. Use the afternoon for anything you missed — Bugis Junction mall, the National Museum of Singapore (free on Friday evenings, SGD 15 weekdays), or just a final laksa before leaving. Many cafes around Bugis and Arab Street are good for a slow afternoon.

  • Evening
    Singapore Zoo Night Safari (if evening flight or flying next day)

    Night Safari opens at 7:15pm (last admission 10pm). Tickets ~SGD 55 (~₹3,450). This is the world's first and best nocturnal wildlife park — 2,500 nocturnal animals in near-natural enclosures. Tram ride is included, then walking trails. Plan 2.5–3 hours. Perfect for a late-night slot before a midnight flight or if you have one more day. Book tickets online to skip the queue. MRT to Khatib, then taxi/Grab (~SGD 12, ₹750).

  • Night
    Changi Airport → Home

    Leave for Changi at least 3 hours before departure for international flights. Use the MRT (~45 min from most areas, ~SGD 1.90). Terminal 2 and 3 have 24-hour food options including a hawker-style food court. One last chicken rice won't hurt.

Without Night Safari: ₹1,500–2,500 With Night Safari: ₹5,000–7,000

Singapore Reality Check

Singapore is expensive — especially accommodation. The biggest way to save is to stay in Little India guesthouses (clean, safe, half the price of Marina Bay) and eat at hawker centres for at least 2 meals a day. A SGD 6 plate of chicken rice from a hawker beats most ₹1,500 restaurant meals in India.

Singapore Transport — What Indian Travellers Need to Know

Singapore's MRT (metro) is one of the best in the world — clean, air-conditioned, punctual, and covers virtually every tourist spot. Buy an EZ-Link card at any MRT station (SGD 12 with SGD 7 credit + SGD 5 deposit, ₹750 total) — it works on MRT, buses, and taxis. Alternatively, a contactless Mastercard or Visa works directly on card readers at MRT gates (no need to even get an EZ-Link). This is the contactless card trick most tourists don't know about.

Grab (Singapore's Uber) is affordable and reliable. Use it for late nights or areas not well-served by MRT (like the Night Safari or some parts of Sentosa).

Transport Tip

Download the Singapore MRT map offline before you go. Cell data can be expensive — pick up a tourist SIM at Changi Airport (Singtel/StarHub tourist SIMs: SGD 15–20 for 100GB, ₹950–1,260) or use Airalo eSIM pre-activated before arrival.

Best Areas to Stay — For Indian Travellers

  • Little India (Mustafa area): Most budget-friendly. Familiar food everywhere. Safe, lively, well-connected. Best for first-timers with limited budgets or those travelling with elders.
  • Chinatown / Tanjong Pagar: Central, great mix of affordable and mid-range hotels. Good food scene. Close to Clarke Quay and Marina Bay by MRT.
  • Clarke Quay / Bugis: Mid-range sweet spot. Vibrant nightlife nearby. Easy MRT access to everywhere.
  • Marina Bay: Premium. The views are incredible but you're paying 3× the price for proximity. Only if budget isn't a concern.
