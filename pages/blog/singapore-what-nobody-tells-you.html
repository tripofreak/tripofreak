Singapore: What Nobody Tells You Before You Go

September 2026 · 12 min read · 12 things guides skip
Share WhatsApp X

Every Singapore travel guide tells you about Marina Bay Sands, Gardens by the Bay, and the hawker centres. What they don't tell you: the fine that'll hit you for eating a packet of chips on the MRT, why your Grab driver might be taking a longer route, and what the "cheap food" promise actually means in practice for a city this expensive. Here are 12 things that will genuinely change how your trip goes.

1
The Rules

The fines are real — and they are enforced, not just signs on walls

Singapore's reputation for strict rules isn't a joke or an exaggeration. Littering: SGD 300–1,000 (₹19,000–63,000) for a first offence. Eating or drinking on the MRT: SGD 500 (₹31,000). Jaywalking within 50 metres of a crossing: SGD 50. Smoking outside designated areas: SGD 200. Officers do patrol, CCTVs are everywhere, and locals report violations. None of this is a problem if you just follow the rules — and it's a big part of why Singapore is so clean and pleasant. But come in knowing: the rules are not decorative.

2
Food Strategy

Hawker centres are the secret — never eat in a mall food court

Singapore has two types of communal eating: hawker centres (government-managed outdoor or semi-outdoor food courts, mostly run by multi-generational family stalls) and mall food courts (commercial, air-conditioned, tourist-facing). The food at hawker centres is better and costs 40–60% less. A plate of Hainanese chicken rice at Maxwell Food Centre: SGD 4–5 (₹250–315). The same dish at a mall food court or tourist restaurant: SGD 12–18 (₹750–1,130). The hawker centre stalls are often run by people who have been making one dish their whole lives. Go there. Every meal.

3
Transport

You don't need a tourist travel card — your contactless bank card works

Most guides tell you to buy an EZ-Link tourist card at the airport. You don't need to. Singapore's MRT and buses accept Visa and Mastercard contactless payment directly — tap your debit or credit card at the gantry, same as a metro in London or Sydney. It automatically charges the correct fare. Works with Apple Pay and Google Pay too. Save yourself the SGD 10 deposit on the tourist card. The only reason to buy one: if your card doesn't have contactless or you want to track spend separately.

4
For Indians

Little India is genuinely Little India — not a tourist version of it

Serangoon Road's Little India is one of the most authentic Indian neighbourhoods outside India. Tamil is spoken on the street, Kollywood music plays from shops, the temples (Sri Veeramakaliamman, Sri Srinivasa Perumal) are active and beautiful, and the food is real — South Indian meals on banana leaves, filter coffee, murukku, idli at 7am. Tekka Centre hawker market here has Indian, Malay, and Chinese stalls in one place. If you're vegetarian, Komala Vilas on Serangoon Road has been serving pure veg South Indian food since 1947 — it's one of the best meals you'll have in Singapore at SGD 8–12 (₹500–750).

5
Sentosa Reality

Sentosa is a theme park island, not a beach getaway

Sentosa is marketed as Singapore's "resort island." What it actually is: an entertainment complex with Universal Studios, beach clubs, a cable car, Resorts World casino, and some mediocre beaches (Siloso, Palawan). The beaches are small, artificial, and surrounded by resort infrastructure — not a tropical island escape. If you're going for Universal Studios with kids, absolutely worth it (SGD 83/adult, ₹5,200 — book online in advance, it's cheaper and avoids queues). If you're expecting a Maldives-style beach day, you'll be disappointed. Spend half a day max unless you have a specific reason to be there.

6
Gardens by the Bay

The outdoor Supertree Grove is free — the domes cost extra and are worth it

Gardens by the Bay has two parts that confuse first-timers. The outdoor Supertree Grove and the park grounds: completely free, open all day. The Supertree light show (Garden Rhapsody) at 7:45pm and 8:45pm: also free. The two indoor domes — Flower Dome and Cloud Forest: SGD 28–53 per person (₹1,750–3,300) depending on which you do. Cloud Forest specifically (a 35-metre indoor mountain with a waterfall) is genuinely spectacular and worth the money. Go for the free outdoor experience in the evening for the light show, and book the Cloud Forest dome for the morning when it's cooler and less crowded.

7
Marina Bay Sands

The MBS infinity pool is hotel guests only — the observation deck is the tourist option

The Marina Bay Sands infinity pool is the most photographed thing in Singapore. It is also exclusively for hotel guests staying at MBS — you cannot buy a day pass. If you want to swim there, you need to book a room (from SGD 500–700/night, ₹31,500–44,000). The SkyPark Observation Deck on the 57th floor is open to the public: SGD 32 (₹2,000) per person, great views. Alternatively, Ce La Vi rooftop bar at MBS is open to the public — buy a drink (SGD 25–35, ₹1,600–2,200) and you get the same views without the entry fee. Evening is best.

8
Weather

It's 32°C and humid every single day — there is no "good season" for weather

Singapore sits 1.3 degrees north of the equator. It is hot and humid 365 days a year with no meaningful cool season. February–April has slightly less rain, making it the "best" time — but it's still 32°C and 80% humidity. Pack light, breathable clothes only. The entire city is air-conditioned indoors (often aggressively cold — carry a light layer for malls and the MRT). Afternoon rain showers hit randomly year-round and pass in 30–60 minutes. Don't let weather dictate your trip — just carry a small umbrella and accept that you will sweat outside.

9
Chinatown

Chinatown is touristy — but the temples and the food are genuinely worth it

Chinatown's Pagoda Street and Temple Street are lined with souvenir shops selling the same keychains and fridge magnets at inflated prices — skip all of it. But the Buddha Tooth Relic Temple on South Bridge Road is genuinely impressive (free entry, remove shoes), the Sri Mariamman Temple next door is the oldest Hindu temple in Singapore (free), and the surrounding back streets have excellent local food. Chinatown Complex Food Centre has over 260 hawker stalls — it's one of the largest in Singapore and has a Michelin-starred hawker selling soya sauce chicken for SGD 3.50 (₹220). Queue before noon or after 2pm to avoid the worst wait.

10
Night Safari

Night Safari is the world's best — but book in advance and go on a weekday

Singapore's Night Safari is legitimately one of the best wildlife experiences in the world — the world's first nocturnal zoo, with animals roaming in open habitats lit only by dim lighting that mimics moonlight. You tram through it and walk select trails. Entry: SGD 55 (₹3,450) adult, SGD 38 (₹2,390) child. The problem: weekend crowds are brutal, with queues for the tram stretching 45–60 minutes. Go on a Tuesday or Wednesday. Book tickets online (10% cheaper and skips the ticket counter). Arrive at opening (7:30pm) — the animals are most active in the first two hours. It runs until midnight.

11
Budget Reality

Singapore is expensive — but hawker food + MRT makes it survivable on a budget

Here's the honest daily cost breakdown: Hawker meals (3/day): SGD 12–18 (₹750–1,130). MRT transport: SGD 5–8 (₹315–500). Attractions (amortised): SGD 20–40 (₹1,260–2,500). Water, snacks, incidentals: SGD 5–10 (₹315–630). Total on-ground: SGD 42–76/day (₹2,600–4,800) if you eat at hawkers, use the MRT, and don't drink alcohol. Add SGD 15–25 per drink if you go to a bar or rooftop. Accommodation is the killer — budget hostels start at SGD 30–40/night (₹1,900–2,500) per person, budget hotels SGD 100–150 (₹6,300–9,450). Singapore rewards people who plan ahead.

12
Timing

4–5 days is the sweet spot — 3 is too rushed, 7+ and you'll run out of things to do

Singapore is a small city-state — 50 km wide, 27 km north to south. You can cross it in 45 minutes on the MRT. Most travellers find they've hit all the major highlights by Day 4. 4 days: Marina Bay area, Gardens by the Bay, Sentosa/Universal, Little India, Chinatown, Night Safari — done comfortably. 5 days: add Pulau Ubin island (a quiet kampong village, very different from city Singapore) or the Southern Ridges walk. The Honest Verdict

Singapore is the easiest international trip you can take from India. Zero language barrier, zero scam risk, excellent food, world-class transport. The price is what it is — expensive — but go in knowing that and you'll leave having had one of the most polished travel experiences of your life. The fines keep the city clean. The hawker centres keep it real. And Little India will make you feel strangely at home 5 hours from Mumbai.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the fines in Singapore tourists should know about?+
Key fines: littering SGD 300–1,000 (₹19,000–63,000), eating or drinking on the MRT SGD 500, jaywalking SGD 50, smoking outside designated areas SGD 200. These are enforced — not just signs. Follow the rules and you'll never encounter them. Chewing gum is not a fine but cannot be imported for sale.
Is Sentosa worth it in Singapore?+
Worth it if you're doing Universal Studios (especially with kids) or a specific beach club. Not worth it as a standalone "beach day" — the beaches are small and artificial. Budget half a day. Universal Studios entry: SGD 83/adult (₹5,200) — book online in advance for the best price.
What is the best hawker centre in Singapore?+
Maxwell Food Centre for Hainanese chicken rice and variety. Old Airport Road Food Centre for locals. Tekka Centre in Little India for Indian food. Chinatown Complex for sheer size and the Michelin-starred hawker. Avoid mall food courts — same food, significantly higher prices.
Is Singapore vegetarian friendly for Indians?+
Very. Little India has dozens of pure vegetarian restaurants. Komala Vilas on Serangoon Road (open since 1947) is the most famous — South Indian thali on a banana leaf for SGD 8–12. Most hawker centres have at least one Indian or vegetarian stall. You will not struggle to find vegetarian food here.
Do I need a tourist SIM for Singapore?+
Not necessarily. Indian carrier international roaming (Airtel, Jio) works well in Singapore and costs ₹500–1,000 for 7 days. Alternatively, airport SIM cards cost SGD 15–25 (₹950–1,600) for 7 days with data. Singapore also has excellent free WiFi in MRT stations, malls, and most hawker centres.
