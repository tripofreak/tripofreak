11 Indian Road Trips
Better Than The Destination

Most Indians plan trips around destinations. Book flights, reach fast, tick boxes, fly back. But some of India's best experiences aren't at the destination — they're on the road getting there. The Gata Loops in Ladakh. The Konkan coastline on NH66. The forests turning gold above Chopta in October. These aren't things you see from a plane window. These 11 road trips will change how you travel — and make you question every flight you've ever booked.

01
Mountain Pass
Manali to Leh
Distance479 km
Drive time2–3 days driving
Best timeJune – September
Budget/person₹15,000–25,000

This is the road trip that ruins all other road trips. Five high-altitude passes, barren moonscape, turquoise rivers, and the Gata Loops — 21 consecutive hairpin bends that feel like a video game. The landscape shifts so dramatically that you'll stop every 20 minutes just to stare.

Key stops: Rohtang Pass, Baralacha La, Sarchu plains, Tanglang La (second highest motorable road in the world). Camp at Sarchu — waking up at 4,290m with stars that close is not something you forget.

Freaky Find: The road officially opens around June 1. Book accommodation in Jispa and Sarchu before you go — there are very few options and they fill up. Don't wing it here.

02
Coastal
Mumbai to Goa — NH66
Distance600 km
Drive timeBest over 2 days
Best timeOctober – February
Budget/person₹8,000–12,000

Everyone flies Mumbai–Goa. Nobody drives it. The coastal NH66 hugs the Konkan shoreline through empty beaches, cashew plantations, and fish thalis at highway dhabas that beat any restaurant in South Goa.

Stop at Malvan for the best seafood you'll eat in Maharashtra — small, family-run joints serving sol kadhi and freshly caught fish that ruins restaurant fish forever. Also stop at Ganpatipule temple (literally on the beach) and Tarkarli for the clearest water on India's west coast.

Freaky Find: Kashid Beach (2.5hrs from Mumbai) is cleaner and less crowded than 90% of Goa beaches. If you leave Mumbai on a Friday, this is your first-night stop.

03
High Altitude
Spiti Valley Circuit
Distance450 km loop
Duration7–10 days
Best timeJune – October
Budget/person₹18,000–28,000

Spiti is what Ladakh was 20 years ago — raw, unfiltered, barely touched. The Shimla–Kinnaur–Kaza road is genuinely terrifying: single-lane mountain tracks with 1,000ft drops and no guardrails. Not for nervous drivers. Absolutely for everyone else.

Don't miss: Hikkim (world's highest post office — send a postcard home), Key Monastery perched above the valley at 4,166m, and Chandratal Lake if you can handle the off-road approach. The lake at sunrise is worth every difficult kilometre.

Freaky Find: The Kinnaur side (Shimla route) has sections where the road is literally carved into the cliff face. Go slow. Watch for rockfalls in monsoon-adjacent months.

04
Plantation
Alleppey to Munnar
Distance170 km
DurationBest over 2 days
Best timeSeptember – March
Budget/person₹6,000–10,000

You go from sea level to 1,600m in one drive. The road climbs through rubber plantations, cardamom hills, waterfalls, and mist — arriving in Munnar as the fog rolls in. One of India's most underrated altitude transitions.

Stop at every chai stall in the tea estates. The tea here tastes different — stronger, more earthy, from gardens you can see from the road. Arrive in Munnar at dusk when the valley fills with clouds from below. One of India's best photographs, and most people miss it by reaching too early.

Freaky Find: Don't rush Ernakulam. The old Jewish Quarter near Mattancherry is one of Kerala's most photogenic neighbourhoods and takes 2 hours max.

05
Desert
Jaipur → Jodhpur → Jaisalmer
Distance580 km
Duration2–3 days
Best timeOctober – February
Budget/person₹10,000–16,000

The landscape gradually desertifies as you drive west — and watching it happen over 580km is the whole point. By Jaisalmer, you're in golden hour sand dunes that look like a film set. The Thar Desert at sunset from a moving car is something no postcard captures.

Drive through Thar villages at dusk. Stop at Osian — an ancient temple complex between Jodhpur and Jaisalmer that almost nobody visits. Skip the touristy Sam Sand Dunes and go to Khuri instead — smaller, quieter, and the camel herders are actually local.

Freaky Find: Pushkar is 45 minutes off the main route. If you're passing, do it — but go early morning before the tourist rush hits the ghats.

06
Coffee Country
Coorg to Chikmagalur
Distance120 km
DurationBest over 3 days
Best timeOct–Feb or June–July
Budget/person₹7,000–11,000

Both districts are coffee country, but they feel completely different. Coorg is lush and orange-scented with gentle hills. Chikmagalur is wilder, higher, with better trekking and fewer tourists. The road connecting them winds through estates where you can stop, talk to farmers, and buy freshly roasted coffee for ₹400/kg — a third of what it costs in Bangalore.

In monsoon (June–July), this drive is extraordinarily green — every waterfall is running, the mist sits low on the estates. Mullayanagiri (Karnataka's highest peak at 1,930m) is 45 minutes from Chikmagalur town and takes 2 hours to climb.

Freaky Find: Baba Budangiri dargah — a cave shrine shared by Hindus and Muslims, halfway up a mountain, almost never crowded. One of Karnataka's most atmospheric sites.

07
Northeast
Ziro Valley to Mechuka
Distance350 km
Duration2–3 days
Best timeOctober – April
Budget/person₹14,000–20,000

Almost nobody does this drive. The roads are rough, the Inner Line Permit takes planning, and the payoff is one of India's most untouched landscapes. Mechuka — a valley near the China border in West Siang — looks like Tibet. The Siyom River, the monastery, the silence. This is what most of India will never look like again.

The Apatani tribal villages around Ziro are a UNESCO Tentative List site — the terraced paddy fields and traditional nose plugs worn by older Apatani women are something no other part of India has. September: Ziro Music Festival — one of India's best kept festival secrets.

Freaky Find: Apply for Inner Line Permits at least 2 weeks early. Accommodation in Mechuka is very limited — book directly with local homestays via phone. No OTA booking here.

08
Coastal
Pondicherry to Rameswaram
Distance380 km
DurationBest over 2 days
Best timeNovember – February
Budget/person₹6,000–9,000

The Pamban Bridge approach is one of India's most dramatic drives — you're crossing a road-railway bridge over open sea onto Pamban Island. On a clear day, Sri Lanka is visible on the horizon. The coastal road through small Tamil fishing towns is completely off the tourist trail.

Stop at Velankanni — a Catholic basilica that draws pilgrims of all faiths, right on the beach. And Chidambaram's Nataraja temple — one of Tamil Nadu's most architecturally extraordinary temples, with corridors that seem to go on forever.

Freaky Find: Adam's Bridge (Ram Setu) viewpoint near Dhanushkodi — the last point of India before the ocean. Drive to the end of the road at Dhanushkodi (a ghost town since the 1964 cyclone) for one of India's most surreal landscapes.

09
Northeast
Guwahati to Tawang
Distance540 km
Duration3–4 days one way
Best timeMarch–June, Sep–Nov
Budget/person₹16,000–24,000

The Sela Pass (4,170m) in winter is one of India's most surreal drives — ice on both sides, frozen lakes, clouds sitting below you on both sides of the ridge. Tawang Monastery at 3,000m is the largest Buddhist monastery in India and the second largest in Asia. The drive to get there is the context that makes it meaningful.

Don't skip Bomdila — a small town with monastery views of the snow-capped mountains that rival anything in Ladakh. Less photographed. More real.

Freaky Find: Bumla Pass near the China border is accessible with a special permit from the army (arranged through local agencies in Tawang). It's 37km from Tawang and closes in winter. Standing at the India–China border is something else entirely.

10
Heritage
Hampi to Badami
Distance150 km
DurationBest over 3 days
Best timeOctober – February
Budget/person₹5,000–8,000

This short drive covers 2,000 years of Indian history. Hampi's boulder-strewn landscape is unlike anywhere in Asia — enormous granite boulders balanced on each other like a giant's abandoned toy set. Badami's cave temples are carved directly into red sandstone cliffs, overlooking a green lake.

The drive through Pattadakal and Aihole — both UNESCO World Heritage sites, both almost empty of tourists — makes this a complete circuit. Most people visit one or the other. The three together, in one drive, is Karnataka's best kept secret.

Freaky Find: Climb the Hemakuta Hill in Hampi at sunrise. You'll be almost alone. By 8am, it's crowded. The difference is enormous.

11
Himalayan
Chopta to Kedarnath via Tungnath
Distance80 km drive + trek
Duration4 days recommended
Best timeMay–June, Sep–Nov
Budget/person₹8,000–13,000

The road from Ukhimath to Chopta in autumn is straight through gold and crimson forests. Chopta is called the "Meadow of Uttarakhand" — a high-altitude meadow at 2,700m with unobstructed Himalayan views. Camping here with Nanda Devi, Trishul, and Chaukhamba peaks visible on a clear night is an experience very few Indians have had.

Tungnath temple (3,680m) is the highest Shiva temple in the world — a 3.5km trek from Chopta. Do it in the early morning before clouds roll in. Combine with Chandrashila summit (4,130m) for 360° Himalayan panorama.

Freaky Find: Deoria Tal — a lake 3km from the road near Sari village that reflects the Chaukhamba peaks perfectly on calm mornings. Most Kedarnath tourists skip it entirely. You shouldn't.

Planning your road trip
Road trip planning

How to plan your
Indian road trip

Indian road trips require different planning than European or American ones. Roads vary wildly. A 200km drive can take 6 hours in the mountains. Here's what actually matters:

  • Check road conditions before every mountain drive. BRO (Border Roads Organisation) posts updates at bro.gov.in. Landslides and snow close roads without warning. Don't assume open.
  • Double Google Maps ETAs on mountain roads. Google doesn't know about last week's landslide, the road narrowing to one lane, or the army convoy you'll get stuck behind for 40 minutes.
  • Book accommodation before you go in peak season. Ladakh in July and Coorg in December fill up completely. Winging it leaves you in overpriced rooms or driving after dark on mountain roads — both bad.
  • Inner Line Permits take time. Arunachal Pradesh requires 1–2 weeks and specific documentation. Some zones near Ladakh's borders also require permits. Do not leave this last minute.
  • Carry a physical map as backup. Signal drops completely in mountain areas. Offline Google Maps helps but even that has gaps in Northeast India and deep Himachal valleys.
  • Travel with at least one other vehicle on remote routes. Spiti, Arunachal, and Ladakh circuits are not solo-drive routes for inexperienced mountain drivers. Breakdowns happen.

Common questions
FAQ

Questions people
actually ask

Which Indian road trip is best for first-timers?
Mumbai to Goa on NH66. Good roads, coastal scenery, plenty of stops, and manageable in a weekend. No permits, no altitude concerns, no special vehicle required. It's the one that shows you what Indian road trips can be — without the risk.
Do I need an SUV for Indian road trips?
For most routes, no. Manali–Leh and Spiti genuinely need high ground clearance — a Swift or i10 will ground out on rocky sections. Every other route on this list is doable in a hatchback with a careful driver. When in doubt, rent a Bolero or Fortuner locally rather than driving your own car across the country.
What's the cheapest Indian road trip on this list?
Hampi to Badami. Accommodation is cheap (₹800–1,500/night for decent rooms), food is inexpensive, and the 150km loop uses minimal fuel. ₹4,000–6,000 per person for 3 days is realistic if you travel in a group of 3–4.
Are solo road trips safe in India?
Generally yes, especially on the popular routes like Mumbai–Goa, Rajasthan circuit, and Karnataka drives. Share your live location with someone at home. For remote routes — Arunachal, deep Spiti, Ladakh border areas — travel with at least one other vehicle. Not for safety from people, but for mechanical breakdowns in places with no phone signal.
When should I avoid Indian road trips?
Monsoon (July–August) makes mountain roads genuinely dangerous — landslides are frequent in Himachal, Uttarakhand, and Northeast India. Manali–Leh and Spiti close entirely. Coastal and plains drives (Rajasthan, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) are fine year-round, though Kerala and Konkan roads get slippery in peak monsoon.
