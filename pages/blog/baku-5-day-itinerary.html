Baku 5-Day Itinerary for Indian Travellers (2026)

5 Days · 4 Nights Updated: September 2026 Budget: ₹50K–₹1.4L all-in Best for: First-timers & Couples

Baku rewards the traveller who shows up with a plan. The Old City is small but layered with history — you can rush it in 2 hours or spend an entire day getting genuinely lost in it. The day trip to Gobustan and the mud volcanoes is unlike anything in Asia. And the Flame Towers at night are the kind of view you remember for years. This itinerary covers everything without wasting a single day.

Before you travel, read 15 things nobody tells you about Baku — especially tips on the Bolt taxi app, vegetarian food, and the currency exchange trap. Our Baku honest scorecard (8.3/10) breaks down what to expect category by category.

Before You Go — Pre-Trip Checklist

  • ✓
    Apply for ASAN e-Visa at evisa.gov.az — USD 26 (~₹2,180), takes 3 business days. Apply at least 5 days before travel.
  • ✓
    Download Bolt app — set up with your Indian number and international card. Use for all rides in Baku. Never use street taxis.
  • ✓
    Download Google Maps offline — Baku city map. Data can be patchy in some areas.
  • ✓
    Download Google Translate — Azerbaijani offline pack — essential for menus, signage, and communicating at local restaurants.
  • ✓
    Get travel insurance — medical costs without insurance in Azerbaijan can be steep. Check your policy covers non-EU destinations.
  • ✓
    Carry USD 100–150 — backup cash for small vendors and markets that don't take cards. Exchange in the city, not at the airport.
  • ✓
    Pack a European Type C adapter — Azerbaijani plugs are 2-pin round (not compatible with Indian plugs directly).
  • ✓
    Book a SIM at Baku airport — Azercell or Bakcell tourist SIMs available at arrivals. 10GB for ~AZN 10 (~₹500).

The 5-Day Plan at a Glance

The logic: Day 1 is arrival + settling in + first taste of the Old City at golden hour. Day 2 is the full Baku city circuit — Flame Towers area, Highland Park, museums, Boulevard evening. Day 3 is the full day trip out of the city. Day 4 goes deeper into Baku's modern and cultural layers. Day 5 is a slow morning, market run, and departure. This order is deliberate — you hit the day trip on Day 3 when your energy is highest and jet lag is gone.

Day 1 Arrival · Old City First Impressions · Fountain Square Evening
  • Arrival
    Land at Heydar Aliyev International Airport (GYD)

    Immigration is generally smooth — show your e-Visa printout or PDF. Get your SIM card from Azercell or Bakcell counter at arrivals (cash or card, ~AZN 10 for 10GB). Open Bolt and book your ride to the city — do not engage with taxi touts outside arrivals. Bolt to city centre: AZN 5–10 (~₹250–500). Journey: 30 minutes.

  • Afternoon
    Check in + first walk to Fountain Square

    Drop luggage at your hotel. Head to Fountain Square (İçəri Şəhər metro stop) — the social heart of modern Baku. It's a pedestrianised plaza surrounded by European-style buildings, cafes, and the entrance to the Old City walls. Get your bearings. Have tea at one of the outdoor cafes — a flat white or Azerbaijani black tea with armud glass. Cost: AZN 2–4 (~₹100–200).

  • Late Afternoon
    First walk inside the Old City (Icherisheher)

    Enter the Old City through the main gate — no ticket required to walk around. Spend 2 hours getting lost in the narrow stone lanes. Walk past the Maiden Tower (Qız Qalası) exterior — you'll come back to go inside tomorrow. Find the Palace of the Shirvanshahs courtyard. The labyrinthine streets in golden afternoon light are magical. Don't stress about a map — you cannot get seriously lost, the walls form a boundary.

  • Evening
    Dinner near Fountain Square

    Walk 2 blocks off Fountain Square to find local pricing restaurants. Try plov (saffron rice), dolma (stuffed grape leaves), and fresh bread. For vegetarians: ask for etsiz plov (plain rice with dried fruits) and a cheese-herb platter. Budget meal: AZN 8–12 (~₹400–600). Tourist-zone restaurants on Fountain Square: AZN 20–35 — skip these tonight.

    Veg option: Ask for "pendir-göyərti" (cheese & herb platter) + "etsiz plov" + salad. Most restaurants can do this.
Budget Day 1: ₹2,500–4,000 Mid-range Day 1: ₹5,000–8,000
Day 2 Old City Deep Dive · Flame Towers · Highland Park · Baku Boulevard
  • 8–10am
    Old City — Maiden Tower + Palace of Shirvanshahs

    Go early before tour groups arrive. Start with the Maiden Tower (AZN 8, ~₹400) — 5 floors of exhibits on Baku's history, plus a rooftop with stunning 360° city views including the Flame Towers above the old walls. Then walk to the Palace of the Shirvanshahs (AZN 8, ~₹400) — a 15th-century palace complex beautifully preserved, with an inner courtyard, mausoleum, and mosque. Budget 2.5 hours for both.

  • Late Morning
    Old City craft shops + çayxana breakfast

    Browse the Old City craft shops — copperware, miniature paintings, silk carpets. Prices are tourist-inflated but the craftsmanship is genuine. Stop at a çayxana (teahouse) inside the Old City for breakfast: Azerbaijani tea with fresh bread, local honey, and white cheese. Cost: AZN 4–6 (~₹200–300). A slow, perfect morning meal.

  • Afternoon
    Flame Towers area + Heydar Aliyev Avenue

    Take a Bolt to the Flame Towers complex (AZN 2–3). The three glass skyscrapers are best experienced up close — the scale is enormous. Walk the surrounding area: the SOCAR Tower (another futuristic glass building), Heydar Aliyev Avenue (Baku's grand boulevard lined with government buildings and fountains). The Fairmont lobby inside the Flame Towers is free to walk through — impressive architecture worth seeing from inside.

  • 5–7pm
    Highland Park (Şəhidlər Xiyabanı) for sunset

    Take a Bolt or walk up to Highland Park — the clifftop park above the Old City that provides the best panoramic view of Baku, the bay, and the Flame Towers at sunset. The park is also a memorial to victims of the January 1990 Soviet crackdown ("Black January") — take a moment to understand the history. The eternal flame memorial is moving. Views are free.

  • Evening
    Baku Boulevard night walk + Flame Towers light show

    Walk down from Highland Park to the Baku Boulevard (Bulvar) along the Caspian waterfront. The evening walk from the Old City end to National Flag Square is 3.5km — take it slowly. The Flame Towers LED display begins at dusk and continues all night, changing colours and animations. This is Baku's great free experience. Grab street food along the Boulevard: corn, fresh juices, local pastries (AZN 1–3 each). Dinner at a Boulevard-adjacent restaurant — budget AZN 15–25 (~₹750–1,250).

Budget Day 2: ₹3,500–5,500 Mid-range Day 2: ₹7,000–11,000
Day 2 Photo Tip

For the iconic Flame Towers reflection shot: stand on the Baku Boulevard facing north at dusk — the towers reflect off the Caspian when the water is calm. Golden hour (30 minutes after sunset) gives the best combination of ambient light and building illumination. Bring a tripod or use a railing for stability.

Day 3 Day Trip — Gobustan · Mud Volcanoes · Ateshgah Fire Temple
  • 8am
    Hire a driver for the day

    This day requires a private driver — no direct public transport to these sites. Arrange through your hotel the evening before, or ask Bolt drivers directly (some offer day-hire). Cost: AZN 50–70 for the full day (~₹2,500–3,500) covering all three sites. Split this among 2–4 people and it's extremely cheap. Agree on the route: Gobustan → Mud Volcanoes → Ateshgah → back to Baku. Leave by 8am to beat the heat.

  • 9:30am
    Gobustan National Park (65km south of Baku)

    Entry: AZN 8 (~₹400) including museum. The museum is genuinely excellent — interactive displays on the 6,000-year-old rock carvings (petroglyphs) that cover the site. The carvings show boats, animals, people, and hunting scenes from the Stone Age. After the museum, walk the outdoor rock paths to see the petroglyphs in situ. Budget 1.5 hours total. The landscape is otherworldly — flat volcanic rock, desert scrubland, fossils.

  • 11:30am
    Mud Volcanoes (12km from Gobustan on unpaved road)

    Azerbaijan has the highest concentration of mud volcanoes on Earth — over 350 of the world's 700. The Gobustan mud volcanoes are small, gently bubbling craters of cool grey mud that look like something from another planet. Free to visit (no entry gate). Budget 30–40 minutes. The road to reach them is rough and unpaved — confirm your driver's car can handle it before the day trip. Wear shoes you don't mind getting muddy.

  • 1:30pm
    Lunch at a roadside restaurant on the way back

    Ask your driver to stop at a local highway restaurant near Gobustan or Ateshgah — these are used by Azerbaijanis, not tourists, and serve excellent food at a fraction of Baku prices. Plov, kebab, fresh bread: AZN 5–8 (~₹250–400) per person. Vegetarians: specify etsiz (no meat) and point to bread, salad, and egg dishes.

  • 3pm
    Ateshgah Fire Temple (30km from Baku)

    Entry: AZN 8 (~₹400). The "House of Fire" is a Zoroastrian and Hindu fire temple built on a natural gas seep — the fire has burned here for centuries, fed by underground gas without any fuel source. What most blogs don't mention: this temple was primarily built and maintained by Indian Hindu and Sikh merchants in the 17th–18th centuries. The Sanskrit and Punjabi inscriptions on the walls, the swastika symbols, and the lingam-shaped structures make this a piece of genuine Indian heritage on Azerbaijani soil. Budget 1 hour. Moving, fascinating, unlike anything in India itself.

    Indian traveller note: Read the Sanskrit inscriptions on the cells — merchants wrote their names and prayers from home. The caretaker can often point out Indian-specific sections if you ask.
  • 5pm
    Return to Baku + rest + Şirvanşah dinner

    Back in Baku by 5–6pm. Rest at the hotel. Tonight is the night for dinner inside the Old City at Şirvanşah Muzey Restoran — a 15th-century caravanserai turned restaurant. The setting is extraordinary: stone arches, candlelight, centuries-old walls. The food is excellent traditional Azerbaijani. Budget AZN 30–50 per person (~₹1,500–2,500). Worth every rupee for one special dinner.

Budget Day 3: ₹4,000–6,000 Mid-range Day 3: ₹7,500–12,000
Day 4 Heydar Aliyev Center · Carpet Museum · Taza Bazar · Nizami Street
  • Morning
    Heydar Aliyev Center — Zaha Hadid's masterpiece

    Designed by Zaha Hadid and opened in 2012, this is one of the most architecturally significant buildings in the world — a flowing white structure with no straight lines, no corners, seemingly defying gravity. Entry to the permanent exhibitions: AZN 15 (~₹750). The building exterior alone is worth the Bolt ride there. Inside: exhibitions on Azerbaijani history, art, and the Heydar Aliyev period. Budget 2 hours. The surrounding landscaped plaza is free and beautiful for photography.

  • Late Morning
    Azerbaijan Carpet Museum

    Entry: AZN 10 (~₹500). The building itself is shaped like a giant rolled carpet. Inside: the world's largest collection of Azerbaijani carpets — 14,000 items, with a curated display showing the regional differences in pattern, colour, and technique across Azerbaijan's carpet-weaving traditions. If you're considering buying a carpet, this is essential context — you'll understand what you're looking at and what makes one carpet worth AZN 100 and another worth AZN 2,000. The museum shop sells authenticated carpets at fair prices. Budget 1.5 hours.

  • Afternoon
    Taza Bazar (central market) + lunch

    Baku's central market is the antidote to Old City tourist pricing. A vast covered bazaar selling fresh produce, dried fruits, nuts, spices, fresh bread, cheese, pickles, and local goods at local prices. This is where Baku residents actually shop. Wander the stalls, taste the dried figs, buy Absheron saffron (AZN 3–5 per gram, ~₹150–250 — far cheaper than tourist shops). Lunch at a market-side restaurant: plov + bread + salad + tea for AZN 5–7 (~₹250–350). Bring your bargaining energy — prices are usually fixed but vendors give better deals if you buy multiple items.

    Veg paradise moment: Taza Bazar has the best pure vegetarian options in Baku — fresh produce stalls, bread bakeries, cheese vendors. Stock up on dried fruits and nuts for snacking.
  • Late Afternoon
    Nizami Street pedestrian walk + shopping

    Nizami Street (also called Torgovaya) is Baku's main pedestrianised shopping street — a 1km stretch of European-style architecture, boutique shops, cafes, and people-watching. Not Bangkok-level shopping, but good for branded clothing, local souvenirs at better prices than the Old City, and excellent cafe-hopping. Licensed currency exchange offices here give near-market rates.

  • Evening
    National Museum of History (optional) + farewell dinner

    The National History Museum on Niyazi Street is housed in a beautiful early 20th-century mansion — AZN 5 (~₹250) entry, good overview of Azerbaijani history from the Silk Road era to Soviet period to independence. Worth 1 hour if you're interested. For farewell dinner: try a modern Azerbaijani restaurant like Vino e Cultura or Neft Bar for the contemporary Baku dining experience — fusion of traditional flavours in a modern setting, AZN 20–35 per person.

Budget Day 4: ₹3,000–5,000 Mid-range Day 4: ₹7,000–12,000
Day 5 Slow Morning · Last Bites · Souvenir Run · Departure
  • Morning
    Slow breakfast at a neighbourhood çayxana

    On your last morning, skip the hotel breakfast and find a local teahouse near Fountain Square or your hotel. Order the full Azerbaijani breakfast spread: fresh bread, white cheese, honey, jam, soft-boiled eggs, tomato-cucumber salad, and black tea in armud glasses. Cost: AZN 6–10 (~₹300–500). One of the most satisfying meals of the entire trip — simple, fresh, and completely unpretentious.

  • Late Morning
    Check out + store luggage + final souvenir run

    Most hotels allow luggage storage until your flight. Use the morning to pick up any remaining souvenirs. Best buys at Taza Bazar: Absheron saffron, dried mulberries, walnut halva, and local tea. At Old City craft shops: hand-engraved copper tea holders (armud holders), small kilim pieces, miniature carpet paintings. For carpets: only buy from the Carpet Museum shop or a registered dealer — ask for an authenticity certificate for anything over AZN 100.

  • Afternoon
    Final Fountain Square coffee + departure

    One last coffee at your favourite Fountain Square cafe. Collect your bags from the hotel. Take Bolt to the airport — book at least 90 minutes before check-in opens (3+ hours before departure for international flights). Bolt from city centre to airport: AZN 5–10 (~₹250–500), 30 minutes. Airport has good duty-free (Azerbaijani caviar, wine, saffron) and food options post-security.

Budget Day 5: ₹2,000–3,500 Mid-range Day 5: ₹4,000–7,000
If You Have More Time

Don't pad Day 5 with extra sightseeing in Baku — you've seen the main things. If you have 7–10 days total, add Georgia (Tbilisi) — Indians get visa-free entry, it's 5 hours by road, and the contrast in culture, food (much better for vegetarians), and landscape is stunning. Where to Stay in Baku

The best areas for Indian travellers: Old City / Fountain Square area (walkable to everything, great for first-timers) and Nizami Street / city centre (central, slightly more modern feel). The Boulevard area is good for premium hotels with Caspian views.

Guesthouses near Old City
₹2,000–4,000/night
Family-run guesthouses inside or adjacent to the Old City walls. Clean, atmospheric, often include breakfast. Ask for a room with a window — some inside rooms are dark.

₹5,000–9,000/night
Excellent 4-star hotels in Baku are genuinely good value — comparable quality to ₹15,000 hotels in Bangkok. Look for Baku Inn, Diplomat Hotel, or similar properties on Booking.com.
Premium

₹18,000–40,000/night
World-class luxury at lower prices than equivalent Dubai properties. The Fairmont is inside the Flame Towers — the view from upper rooms at night is surreal. The Four Seasons overlooks the Caspian.
Booking Tip

Book 4–6 weeks in advance for September–October (peak season). Check both Booking.com and the hotel's direct website — Baku hotels sometimes have better rates on their own site. Always read recent reviews filtering for "Indian travellers" or "vegetarian" to find genuine notes on food options.

Getting Around Baku

Bolt App (Taxi)

Primary transport for tourists. Cheap, reliable, English-language, rated drivers. City centre to most attractions: AZN 2–6 (~₹100–300). Airport to city: AZN 5–10 (~₹250–500). Set up before landing.

Metro

Clean, fast, AZN 0.30/ride (~₹15). Get a BakıKart (~₹50 deposit). Covers city centre, Fountain Square, Heydar Aliyev Centre area. Announcements in Azerbaijani — use Google Maps for navigation.

Walking

Old City, Fountain Square, Boulevard, and Nizami Street are all walkable from each other. The Old City itself is best on foot — streets too narrow for cars. Wear flat shoes on cobblestones.

Private Driver (Day Trips)

Essential for Gobustan, Mud Volcanoes, and Ateshgah. AZN 50–70 for full day (~₹2,500–3,500 per car). Arrange through your hotel or directly with a Bolt driver the evening before.
