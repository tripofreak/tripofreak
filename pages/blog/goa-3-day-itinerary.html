Before you book — know this
📅Best time: October–February. Avoid May–September (monsoon — most shacks shut, sea is rough)
🛵Rent a scooter or bike — ₹300–500/day. Goa without a vehicle is a frustrating experience. Cabs are 3x more expensive and rarely available where you actually want to go
🏨Book accommodation early — December–January prices double in 72 hours. Book 3+ weeks in advance for Nov–Feb
🍺Alcohol is cheap. Kingfisher at a shack: ₹80–120. Same bottle in Delhi or Mumbai: ₹300+. Don't pre-drink from hotel room minibar
🚫Don't eat at the first shack you see on a crowded beach — it's the most expensive and usually the worst. Walk 100m further
📱Most beaches have spotty 4G. Download offline maps before you leave your hotel

Everyone's been told the same Goa itinerary. Calangute, Baga, Anjuna flea market, a shack sunset, repeat. And it's fine — but it's not Goa. The real version of Goa is about 20 minutes away from where you'll be dropped by your travel agent. This is a 3-day plan for people who want to actually experience the place, not just post it.

Day One
01
North Goa
Arrive, Settle, Explore the Coast
VibeEasygoing
BaseNorth Goa
Est. spend₹1,200–2,500
Morning Arrive and check in. If you're in North Goa, base yourself near Vagator or Anjuna — not Calangute. Better vibe, less chaos, same prices. Grab breakfast at a local café. Martin's Corner is overrated and far; try any small place on the Vagator road — egg bhurji and chai for ₹80.
Afternoon Hit Vagator beach. Specifically, walk down to Little Vagator (Ozran Beach) — down the steps, not the main entry. Fewer people. The red laterite cliffs behind the beach are striking in afternoon light. This is also where Shiva Café is: overpriced drinks, unbeatable sunset view from the clifftop.
Sunset Chapora Fort. 15 minutes from Vagator. The views over the fort walls — red cliffs, green hills, river mouth meeting the sea — are what every Goa sunset photo you've seen is actually of. Get there 30 minutes before sunset. Bring water. There's nothing to buy at the top.
Night Dinner in Anjuna. Gunpowder restaurant does South Indian food that makes no sense to be this good in Goa. Or, if you want seafood, ask your hotel where locals eat — not where the sign says "authentic Goan seafood." That's never where locals eat.
Freaky Find

Avoid Baga on Day 1. It's the most crowded, loudest, and most expensive stretch of North Goa — and fine for a night out later, but brutal when you're jetlagged and just want to settle in. Give yourself a quiet first night so you actually want to stay in Goa for 3 days.

Day Two
02
Culture + Beaches
Old Goa, Hidden Beaches, Night Out
VibeExplore + Party
Distance~40 km total
Est. spend₹1,500–3,500
Morning Old Goa — Basilica of Bom Jesus and Se Cathedral. Most people skip this because it sounds like a history lesson. It isn't. The Portuguese-era churches here are genuinely stunning, and at 9am they're empty. UNESCO heritage, 16th century architecture, golden altars. 45 minutes to an hour, free entry, completely worth it.
Late Morning Fontainhas — Latin Quarter, Panaji. The only place in India that looks like Lisbon. Narrow lanes, brightly painted colonial houses, a black-and-white tiled chapel. Best for photos between 9–11am before it heats up. Grab a Bebinca (traditional Goan dessert) from a bakery here.
Afternoon Morjim or Ashwem beach. North Goa's quieter stretch. Fewer shacks, more space, same sea. Morjim is also an Olive Ridley sea turtle nesting site — you might see markings on the sand around November. Swim here, not at Baga (the current at Baga is rough).
Evening Baga or Tito's Lane if you want the famous Goa night scene. It's loud, it's crowded, it's everything the Instagram stories are. Worth experiencing once. Clubs open properly after 10pm. Wednesday night at Club Cabana if the date lines up.
Freaky Find

Mandovi River cruise — skip it. Every travel agent will push this. It's a tourist trap with bad food and mediocre folk dance on a crowded boat. If you want water, rent a kayak from Panaji riverfront for ₹200/hour. Completely different experience.

Day Three
03
South Goa
Palolem, The Best Beach in Goa
VibeSlow + Beautiful
Drive~2 hrs from North Goa
Est. spend₹1,000–2,000
Early Morning Leave North Goa by 7–8am. Palolem is 2 hours south. The drive through the Western Ghats foothills is genuinely beautiful — you'll pass through spice country, cashew farms, and forests. Don't take the highway; take the old NH66 coastal road through Margao.
Late Morning Palolem beach. A crescent-shaped bay with calm water, fishing boats, and palm trees that actually look like a postcard. Arrive before 10am and you'll have wide stretches of beach to yourself. The water here is calmer than North Goa — safe to swim without watching the flags.
Afternoon Agonda beach — 10 minutes further south. Even quieter than Palolem. Very few tourists, no jet skis, no hawkers. Just beach. If you need a day 3 that undoes the noise of days 1 and 2, this is it. A handful of good beach shacks for lunch.
Before leaving Fish thali at any local joint in Margao on the way back. Not a shack. A restaurant. Prawn curry rice, sol kadhi, papad, pickle — ₹150–200. This is the actual Goan food that locals eat every day and that no travel guide ever sends you to.
Freaky Find

Butterfly Beach — only if you have a kayak or boat. It's 20 minutes by boat from Palolem and accessible only by water. No shacks, no vendors, no crowd. Rent a kayak from Palolem (₹500–700) and paddle there in the morning. The most photogenic beach in Goa that 90% of visitors never see.

What Most People Miss
Things Nobody Tells You

The Real Goa is Off the Shack Menu

Every list of "Goa tips" tells you the same things: rent a scooter, try Goan fish curry, watch the sunset from a cliff. All true. But here's what those lists leave out.

  • Tuesday market at Anjuna is better than Wednesday: Less crowded, same stuff, half the pushy vendors. Wednesday Anjuna flea market is infamous for overcharging tourists.
  • Goa has a monsoon season that's actually beautiful: July–September, almost everything shuts. But the waterfalls (Dudhsagar especially) are only accessible after the rains — October is the sweet spot before crowd season.
  • The spice farms near Ponda are worth half a day: ₹400–600/person, includes a full lunch and a walking tour. Far better than any museum and most beaches for something genuinely different.
  • Uber barely works in Goa: Download the GoaMiles app, or just rent a scooter. Cabs demand flat rates (₹500–800 for trips that cost ₹150 on a meter). Negotiate before getting in.
  • The most photographed sunset spot isn't what you think: Chapora Fort gets you the actual view. "Sunset point" near Candolim is a car park with a crowd. Don't confuse them.
  • Swimming safety: Red or yellow flags mean no swimming. In peak season, North Goa beaches have strong undertow. Palolem and Agonda (South Goa) are calmer, safer, better for swimming.

Goa in 3 Days — Answered

Is 3 days enough for Goa?
Yes — 3 days is the sweet spot for Goa. Enough to see North and South Goa, hit the best beaches, try the food, and catch at least one proper sunset. More than 5 days and you're repeating yourself. 3 days keeps it fresh.
North Goa or South Goa — which is better for 3 days?
Do both. Base yourself in North Goa (Vagator or Anjuna area) for days 1–2, and do a day trip to South Goa (Palolem) on day 3. North Goa = energy, nightlife, crowds. South Goa = calm, cleaner beaches, better swimming. The contrast is worth the 2-hour drive.
What is the budget for a 3-day Goa trip?
Budget: ₹8,000–10,000 per person (hostel, local food, scooter). Mid-range: ₹14,000–18,000 (hotel, shack meals, some activities). Luxury: ₹25,000+ (resort, experiences, eating out every meal). Excludes flights or train.
What should I not miss in Goa?
Chapora Fort at sunset (not Anjuna market). Palolem beach in South Goa. Fish thali at a local restaurant (not a tourist shack). Fontainhas in Panaji. And at least one morning at a beach before 8am — before the crowds, the music, and the vendors.
What is the best time to visit Goa?
October to February. January is ideal — perfect weather, sea is calm, fewer crowds than December. Avoid May–September (monsoon, most beach shacks shut). October is an underrated window: waterfalls are full, beaches are empty, prices are low.
