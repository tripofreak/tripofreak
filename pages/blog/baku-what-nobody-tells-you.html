15 Things Nobody Tells You About Baku, Azerbaijan

15 Tips · Honest Guide Updated: September 2026 For: Indian Travellers Read time: 14 min

Most Baku travel content is either a tourism board press release or a vague list of "top 10 attractions." Neither tells you what to actually expect as an Indian traveller — why vegetarians struggle, why Ateshgah matters more to us than to anyone else, why the summer heat will ruin your trip, and the one app that will save you ₹3,000 over five days.

These 15 tips are the things we wish someone had told us before we booked. Read our Baku honest scorecard (8.3/10) for the full category-by-category breakdown, and our Baku 5-day itinerary for the day-by-day plan.

The 60-Second Summary

  • Use Bolt app — never a street taxi. Saves ₹2,000–4,000 per trip.
  • The Old City (Icherisheher) is free to enter. Most tourists pay for tours they don't need.
  • Ateshgah Fire Temple was built by Indian merchants — it's literally our heritage.
  • July–August is brutal (40°C). Visit April–June or September–October.
  • Vegetarians: say "etsiz" (without meat). Plan at least one Indian restaurant per day.
  • Gobustan day trip takes a half-day, not a full day. Combine it with Mud Volcanoes.
  • Baku + Georgia (Tbilisi) is a perfect 9-10 day 2-country trip.
01
The Bolt App Is Non-Negotiable — Delete the Idea of Street Taxis
💰 Saves Money ⚠️ Avoid Mistake

This is tip #1 for a reason. The moment you step out of Baku Heydar Aliyev Airport, taxi drivers will approach you offering "fixed price" rides to the city. The prices they quote range from AZN 30–60 (~₹1,500–3,000). The actual Bolt price for the same ride: AZN 5–10 (~₹250–500).

Download Bolt (similar to Uber) before you land. Set it up with your Indian number and an international card. The app works perfectly in Baku, drivers are rated, cars are clean, and there's no negotiating. Use it for every ride — from the airport to your hotel, between attractions, back from the night out.

Over five days, an average traveller takes 15–20 trips. At Bolt rates vs. street taxi rates, you'll save ₹3,000–5,000 easily — enough for two nights of a decent hotel or five excellent restaurant meals.

→ Airport to city centre by Bolt: ~₹350–500. Street taxi: ₹1,500–3,000. Use Bolt.
02
Ateshgah Fire Temple Was Built by Indian Merchants — It's Our Heritage
🏛️ Culture 🌟 Unlock Experience

Most travel blogs mention Ateshgah ("House of Fire") as a Zoroastrian temple. What they leave out: it was primarily built and used by Indian Hindu and Sikh merchants who came to Baku for trade during the 17th–18th centuries. The inscriptions inside are in Sanskrit and Punjabi. The fire that burns here eternally — fed by natural gas seeping through the ground — was sacred to both Zoroastrians and Hindus who worshipped Agni.

You'll find swastika symbols (the ancient Hindu symbol for prosperity, not the Nazi inversion), Sanskrit scripts, and lingam-shaped structures inside the main complex. As an Indian visiting Baku, this isn't just a tourist attraction — it's a piece of Indian cultural heritage sitting in Azerbaijan, largely forgotten by us.

Entry fee: AZN 8 (~₹400). Located 30km from central Baku — combine with the Gobustan day trip. Hire a driver for both (AZN 30–40 total, ~₹1,500–2,000 split among 2–4 people).

→ Read the Sanskrit inscriptions on the inner walls. The temple caretakers often know more about the Indian connection than most visitors.
03
The Old City (Icherisheher) Is Free to Enter — You Don't Need a Guide or Tour
💰 Saves Money 🌟 Unlock Experience

Baku's Old City is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the single most impressive thing in Azerbaijan. The medieval walls, narrow stone lanes, the Maiden Tower, the Palace of the Shirvanshahs — it feels like stepping into a 12th-century Islamic city that somehow survived. There are no entrance fees to walk around the Old City itself.

Tourists routinely pay ₹1,500–2,500 for guided tours of the Old City that add nothing you can't get from a good map or a free audio guide. What you should actually pay for:

  • Maiden Tower (Qız Qalası): AZN 8 (~₹400) entry — 5 floors of exhibits + rooftop view. Worth it.
  • Palace of the Shirvanshahs: AZN 8 (~₹400) — beautifully preserved 15th-century palace complex. Go.

Budget 4–5 hours for a proper Old City wander including both paid sites. Come in the morning (8–10am) when the light is golden and tour groups haven't arrived yet. The narrow lanes at dawn are genuinely magical.

→ The best free view of the Flame Towers is from the Old City walls — no ticket needed, just walk up.
04
July and August Will Ruin Your Trip — The Heat Is Genuinely Brutal
⚠️ Avoid Mistake

Baku sits in an arid, semi-desert microclimate on the Caspian Sea. In July and August, temperatures regularly hit 38–42°C. Unlike coastal tropical heat, there's almost no sea breeze in the city — the Caspian doesn't generate wind the way an ocean does. The Old City's narrow stone lanes trap heat like an oven.

Several Indian travellers who visit in peak summer report spending most of their time in air-conditioned malls and hotels rather than sightseeing. Outdoor day trips to Gobustan and Mud Volcanoes become miserable, and the Flame Towers at sunset (which you walk to and from) are a sweaty ordeal.

Best months: April–May (18–26°C, blooming spring) and September–October (22–30°C, golden autumn light, clear skies). The shoulder period is also when flights and hotels are slightly cheaper.

→ If you must go in summer, schedule all outdoor sightseeing before 10am and after 5pm. Midday in Baku in August is dangerous-level heat.
05
Vegetarians: Have a Strategy Before You Land
⚠️ Avoid Mistake

Azerbaijani cuisine is built around meat — lamb, beef, chicken, and fish feature in almost every traditional dish. The national staple, plov, is cooked in meat stock even when it doesn't contain visible meat. Soups and stews are almost always meat-based. This is not a hostile situation; it's just a cultural reality.

What vegetarians can eat without stress:

  • Bread (çörək): Azerbaijan's tendir bread (baked in clay tandoor ovens) is exceptional. Available everywhere, always veg.
  • Cheese and herb platter (pendir və göyərti): A standard starter — fresh white cheese, tomatoes, cucumbers, herbs. Usually veg.
  • Plov without meat (etsiz plov): Ask specifically — some places will make plain saffron rice with dried fruits and chestnuts. Genuinely delicious.
  • Dolma with vegetables: The grape leaf-wrapped version can be made vegetarian — ask.
  • Salads: Azerbaijani salads (tomato, cucumber, herb) are reliably veg.

Indian restaurants in Baku (look up current ones near your hotel): There are several Indian restaurants in central Baku — they're not cheap by local standards but are a lifesaver for strict vegetarians. Plan at least one Indian meal per day on a veg-only trip.

→ Learn these two words: "etsiz" (without meat) and "vegetarian" (same in Azerbaijani, they'll understand). Show it written on your phone.
06
Gobustan + Mud Volcanoes Is a Half-Day — Not a Full Day
🌟 Unlock Experience 💰 Saves Money

Gobustan National Park, 65km south of Baku, contains 6,000-year-old rock carvings (petroglyphs) — one of the best prehistoric art sites in the world. The Mud Volcanoes nearby are bizarre, otherworldly, and unlike anything you've seen (Azerbaijan has more mud volcanoes than anywhere else on Earth). Both are must-dos.

The reality: Gobustan takes 1.5 hours including the museum. The Mud Volcanoes take 30–45 minutes. Drive time from Baku is 1–1.5 hours each way. Total time: 5–6 hours. This is a morning-only activity, not a full day.

Many tour operators sell "full day Gobustan tours" for ₹3,000–4,000 per person that pad the schedule with unnecessary stops. Instead: hire a private driver through your hotel or through Bolt's "book a trip" feature (some drivers do day hires). Cost: AZN 40–60 (~₹2,000–3,000) for the car total, split among your group. Far cheaper and you control the schedule.

Combine with Ateshgah Fire Temple on the way back (it's on the same route) for an efficient full morning.

→ The Mud Volcanoes are 12km from the Gobustan visitor centre on a rough unpaved road. Make sure your hired car/driver can handle it — confirm before hiring.

07
The Flame Towers Are Free to See — and Best After Dark
💰 Saves Money 🌟 Unlock Experience

Baku's most iconic landmark — the three Flame Towers — are 182-metre glass skyscrapers whose entire facade is an LED display. After dark, they literally simulate burning flames across the city skyline. It's one of the most genuinely spectacular urban light shows in the world, and it's completely free to watch.

The best viewing spots, all free:

  • Highland Park (Şəhidlər Xiyabanı): The clifftop park above the Old City — best panoramic view of both the towers and the whole bay. Take a taxi up; the park itself is a moving memorial to the January 1990 massacre victims.
  • Baku Boulevard (Bulvar): The 3.5km seaside promenade along the Caspian — beautiful evening walk with Flame Towers backdrop.
  • Old City walls: Looking up from within the Old City, the towers glow orange-red above the medieval minarets. Surreal contrast.

There's a Fairmont hotel inside the Flame Towers complex. You can walk into the lobby for free and look around — the architecture is impressive up close — but you don't need to stay there to appreciate the view.

→ The LED display changes colours on public holidays and national events. September 15 (Independence Day) and November 9 (National Flag Day) have special displays.
08
Never Exchange Currency at the Airport or Hotels
💰 Saves Money ⚠️ Avoid Mistake

Airport exchange counters in Baku offer rates that are 5–8% worse than the mid-market rate. On a ₹50,000 cash exchange, that's ₹2,500–4,000 lost before you've even left the arrivals hall.

Better options:

  • ABB Bank ATMs (green) or PASHA Bank ATMs: Withdraw AZN directly with your international debit card. Use a Niyo Global, HDFC Regalia Forex, or any zero-forex card to avoid the foreign transaction markup. ATMs widely available across the city centre.
  • Central Exchange Offices: Along Nizami Street (Baku's main pedestrian shopping street) and near Fountain Square, licensed exchange offices offer near-market rates. Compare 2–3 offices before exchanging.
  • USD is the best foreign currency to carry: USD exchanges at better rates than EUR in Baku. EUR is also accepted but at slightly worse rates.

Tip: carry USD 100–200 as emergency cash for places that don't take cards (smaller restaurants, market stalls). Most mid-range hotels and restaurants accept Visa/Mastercard.

→ Mastercard generally works better than Visa at Azerbaijani ATMs. If your Visa card gets declined, try an ABB or Kapital Bank ATM specifically.
09
Baku Boulevard Is the Best Free Evening in the City
🌟 Unlock Experience

The Baku Boulevard (Bulvar) is a 3.5km seaside promenade along the Caspian Sea — one of the longest in the world. In the evenings, the entire city comes out: families, couples, teenagers, old men playing backgammon, street musicians. It feels like Marine Drive in Mumbai but quieter, cleaner, and with the Flame Towers glowing behind you.

Walk the full stretch from the Carpet Museum end to the National Flag Square end. The Carpet Museum itself (shaped like a giant rolled carpet) is worth entering — AZN 10 (~₹500), genuinely fascinating Azerbaijani carpet collection. The Baku Eye Ferris wheel on the Boulevard is touristy but the view from the top is good (AZN 5, ~₹250).

The Boulevard has multiple food kiosks and small restaurants along the waterfront — grilled corn, fresh fruit juices, local snacks. Keep your wallet out for ₹50–100 per snack.

→ The best evening walk: Old City → Highland Park for sunset view → Boulevard for night walk → Fountain Square for street food. Takes 3 hours, costs almost nothing.
10
Google Translate (Azerbaijani Offline) Is More Important Than a Phrasebook
⚠️ Avoid Mistake

English is spoken at hotels, major tourist sites, and some upscale restaurants in Baku. Outside those spaces — local teahouses, markets, taxi drivers, smaller restaurants, neighbourhood shops — you'll find limited to no English. Russian is the second language for older Azerbaijanis (Soviet legacy); younger people speak Azerbaijani and some Turkish.

Download these before you leave for Baku:

  • Google Translate → Azerbaijani language pack (offline): Essential. The camera translation feature is a lifesaver for reading menus.
  • Google Maps → Baku offline map: Download before landing. Navigate without burning data.
  • Bolt app: Set up before arrival — you'll need a working number and card.

The Azerbaijani language is Turkic and shares significant vocabulary with Turkish. If you speak any Turkish (even from travel), it helps. Basic courtesies: "Salam" (hello), "Çox sağ ol" (thank you, pronounced "chokh saa-ol"), "Bağışlayın" (excuse me), "Nə qədər?" (how much?).

→ Most Azerbaijani locals are genuinely friendly toward Indians. The language barrier rarely becomes a serious problem — patience and Google Translate get you through.
11
Baku Is Not a Shopping Destination — Manage Expectations
⚠️ Avoid Mistake

If shopping is a priority, Baku will disappoint. There are no Bangkok-style night markets, no Singapore-style malls with good deals on electronics or fashion, and no Moroccan-style souqs with handmade goods. What Baku does have:

  • Azerbaijani carpets: Genuinely world-famous. If you want to buy a quality carpet, this is one of the best places in the world. Prices: AZN 100–2,000+ (~₹5,000–1,00,000) depending on size, age, and region. Buy from the Carpet Museum's affiliated shop for guaranteed authenticity.
  • Saffron: Some of the world's best saffron comes from the Absheron region. Buy at Taza Bazar (central market) — far cheaper than tourist shops. 1 gram of quality saffron: AZN 3–5 (~₹150–250).
  • Dried fruits and nuts: Excellent quality, cheap at the market.
  • Traditional copperware (misgərlik): Hand-engraved copper trays and vessels from the Old City craft shops.

The Old City has souvenir shops but prices are tourist-inflated. The same items at the central bazaar (Taza Bazar) cost 40–60% less.

→ Best souvenir from Baku: a small kilim (flat-weave rug), a packet of Absheron saffron, and a copper tea glass holder (armud). Total cost at Taza Bazar: under AZN 30 (~₹1,500).
12
The Restaurants INSIDE the Old City Are Tourist-Priced — Walk Two Blocks Out
💰 Saves Money

There are excellent restaurants inside the Old City walls — atmospheric, beautiful settings, sometimes genuinely good food. They're also priced 2–3× what you'd pay 200 metres away outside the walls. A plov + kebab + tea meal inside the Old City: AZN 25–40 (~₹1,250–2,000). The same meal at a local teahouse 5 minutes' walk away: AZN 8–15 (~₹400–750).

Where to eat well at local prices:

  • Taza Bazar area: The streets around Baku's central market have dozens of local restaurants, bakeries, and teahouses used by working Azerbaijanis, not tourists.
  • Fountain Square area (side streets): Walk off the main square onto the parallel streets — prices drop immediately.
  • The one exception inside the Old City: Şirvanşah Muzey Restoran — a 15th-century caravanserai turned restaurant. The food is excellent, the atmosphere is extraordinary, and the premium is worth it once. Go for dinner.
→ Ask your hotel for the nearest local teahouse (çayxana) recommendation. These are where Baku residents actually eat — and the food is invariably better and cheaper than tourist-facing restaurants.

13
Baku + Georgia (Tbilisi) Is One of the Best 2-Country Trip Combos for Indians
🌟 Unlock Experience

Georgia and Azerbaijan share a border and are just 5 hours apart by road (Baku to Tbilisi by marshrutka minibus, ~₹800). Many Indian travellers don't realise you can easily combine both countries into one 9–12 day trip — making the long connecting flight from India worthwhile.

The contrast is stunning: Azerbaijan is Islamic, oil-rich, modern; Georgia is Orthodox Christian, wine-producing, mountainous. Tbilisi is one of the most beautiful, underrated cities in the world for Indian travellers — Georgian food is excellent for vegetarians too (khachapuri, lobiani, fresh salads).

Sample routing: Fly into Baku → 5 days Baku + day trips → overnight marshrutka to Tbilisi → 4–5 days Georgia (Tbilisi, Mtskheta, Kazbegi) → fly home from Tbilisi. Indians get visa-free entry to Georgia.

Georgia also offers significantly better vegetarian food than Azerbaijan — a bonus for those struggling in Baku.

→ Baku–Tbilisi flights take 1.5 hours and can be as cheap as USD 30–50 one-way on AZAL or Georgian Airways. Book 3–4 weeks in advance.
14
Baku Has an Incredible Cafe Culture — and It's Surprisingly Good Coffee
🌟 Unlock Experience

Baku's cafe scene is one of the most underreported things about the city. The influence of Turkish and European culture has produced a genuinely good specialty coffee culture — proper pour-overs, espresso, excellent pastries. Prices are a fraction of what you'd pay in Mumbai or Delhi for the same quality.

The Fountain Square and Nizami Street area is lined with cafes — both local Azerbaijani bakeries serving çay (tea) and şirniyyat (sweets), and modern European-style coffee shops. A flat white at a good Baku cafe: AZN 2.5–4 (~₹125–200). Azerbaijani black tea with sugar and jam (çay) at a teahouse: AZN 0.50 (~₹25).

The traditional Azerbaijani tea ritual is worth experiencing — strong black tea served in armud glasses (pear-shaped, so you hold the narrow waist) with rock sugar, jam, and sometimes fresh walnuts. Request it at any çayxana (teahouse).

→ Cafe Muzey inside the Shirvanshahs Palace complex serves Azerbaijani tea with a view of the medieval courtyard. One of the best ₹200 experiences in the city.
15
Baku Is 4–5 Days, Not More — Know When You've Seen It All
💰 Saves Money

Baku is an extraordinary city. It's also a relatively compact city — you will genuinely run out of must-see attractions after day 5. Unlike Bangkok, Tokyo, or even Istanbul where you could spend two weeks and still miss things, Baku's main attractions can be comfortably covered in 4–5 days with day trips included.

What 4–5 days covers: Old City (full day), Flame Towers area + Boulevard + Highland Park, Carpet Museum, National History Museum, Gobustan + Mud Volcanoes + Ateshgah (one day), Taza Bazar, Nizami Street, and a relaxed afternoon at the Caspian waterfront.

Spending 7–10 days in Baku alone will lead to padding your schedule with less interesting things. Use the extra days to add Georgia (as above) or to spend more time at Quba, Sheki, or the Caucasus mountains in northern Azerbaijan — genuinely beautiful areas that most Indian travellers never visit because they focus only on Baku.

→ If you have 8 days: 4 days Baku + 4 days Georgia. If you have 10 days: 4 days Baku + 2 days northern Azerbaijan + 4 days Georgia. That's the optimal Caucasus trip from India.
