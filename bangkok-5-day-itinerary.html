Bangkok 5-Day Itinerary for Indian Travellers (2026)

5 Days · 4 Nights Updated: September 2026 Budget: ₹35K–₹1.2L all-in Visa: Visa-free for Indians

Bangkok rewards a plan. Without one, you spend Day 1 figuring out the BTS, Day 2 stuck in the Grand Palace queue at noon, and Day 3 wandering Sukhumvit because you've run out of ideas. With the right order, you get the temples before the crowds, the river ferry instead of the tourist boat, Ayutthaya with enough time to actually explore it, and an evening in Chinatown that you'll talk about for years.

Before You Go — Pre-Trip Checklist

  • ✓
    Download Grab — set up with your Indian phone number and international card before landing. Never use street taxis or tuk-tuks for actual transport.
  • ✓
    Download Google Maps offline — Bangkok — saves your data and works in the metro where signal can drop.
  • ✓
    Pack temple clothes — one pair of lightweight long trousers and a top that covers your shoulders. Carry a thin scarf as backup for Wat Arun (they're strict about arms).
  • ✓
    Get a Thai SIM at the airport — DTAC or AIS tourist SIMs at arrivals. 15GB for ~THB 299 (~₹690). Essential for Grab and Google Maps.
  • ✓
    Exchange currency after the airport — SuperRich exchange on Ratchadamri Road and Asok area give the best rates. Airport counters are 8–10% worse.
  • ✓
    Book Ayutthaya transport — minivan from Victory Monument (Day 4) or train from Hua Lamphong station. No need to pre-book far ahead but confirm the night before.
  • ✓
    Check if Day 3 falls on a weekend — Chatuchak Market is Saturday and Sunday only. If your Day 3 is a weekday, swap Days 3 and 4 in this plan.

The 5-Day Plan at a Glance

The logic behind this order: Day 1 is arrival and orientation — no rushing, just settling in and getting a feel for the city. Day 2 is the temple circuit while your energy is highest. Day 3 is Chatuchak and the cultural side of Bangkok. Day 4 is the Ayutthaya day trip — the single best thing you can do outside the city. Day 5 is a slow exit. This order means you never waste a fresh morning on something that can wait, and you don't save the temples for when you're tired.

Day 1 Arrival · Sukhumvit Orientation · Chinatown Evening
  • Arrival
    Land at Suvarnabhumi (BKK) — SIM, exchange, Grab to hotel

    Get your SIM card at the arrivals hall (DTAC or AIS counter, ~THB 299). Don't exchange money here — rates are bad. Open Grab and book your ride to the hotel from the designated rideshare zone (follow signs for "Public Transport / Taxi Meter"). Grab to Sukhumvit: THB 200–320 (~₹460–730), 30–50 minutes depending on traffic. Airport Rail Link to Phaya Thai BTS: THB 45, 28 minutes — better if your hotel is near the Skytrain. The full airport transport breakdown — and why you should never take a tuk-tuk for actual city travel — is in our Bangkok tips guide.

  • Afternoon
    Check in + first walk around Sukhumvit

    Drop bags, freshen up, walk the area around your hotel. Sukhumvit Soi 11 and Soi 3 are good starting points — mix of cafes, local restaurants, convenience stores (7-Eleven every 200 metres), and street food carts setting up for the evening. This is just orientation — no agenda, no temples yet. Get a cold Thai iced tea from a street cart (THB 20–30) and start noticing how the city works.

  • Evening
    Chinatown (Yaowarat Road) for dinner

    Take Grab or MRT to Yaowarat — Bangkok's Chinatown and the best street food strip in the city. The road comes alive after 6pm: seafood grills, noodle stalls, roast duck vendors, dessert carts, fresh fruit. For vegetarians, look for yellow เจ flags — Chinatown has more Jay (Buddhist vegan) restaurants than anywhere else in Bangkok. Budget for dinner: THB 150–300 (~₹350–700) per person. Eat a lot — this is one of the best evenings Bangkok offers and it costs almost nothing.

    Veg: Ask for Jay (เจ) noodles or fried rice. Several dedicated Jay restaurants on and just off Yaowarat Road. Look for yellow flags.
  • Night
    Walk the Yaowarat neon strip + return early

    After dinner, walk the length of Yaowarat Road slowly — the neon signs, the gold shop windows, the dessert stalls, the noise. It feels like a different city from Sukhumvit. Don't stay too late — Day 2 starts early. Back to the hotel by 10pm, sleep well.

Budget Day 1: ₹2,000–3,500 Mid-range Day 1: ₹4,500–7,000
Day 2 Grand Palace · Wat Pho · Chao Phraya Ferry · Wat Arun · Silom Night
  • 8:30am
    Grand Palace + Wat Phra Kaew — arrive at opening

    Gates open at 8:30am. Be here then. The first hour is dramatically different from the 10am–2pm crush — you can stop walking, look up at the spires, and actually take in the scale of what you're standing in front of. Entry is THB 500 (~₹1,150) and includes Wat Phra Kaew (the Emerald Buddha temple) inside the compound. Budget 1.5–2 hours. Temple dress is enforced — long trousers and covered shoulders, no exceptions. Sarong rental stalls are right outside if you forgot. See our Bangkok honest scorecard for how the Grand Palace compares to other major Bangkok experiences on a value-for-time basis.

  • 10:30am
    Wat Pho — Reclining Buddha

    Exit the Grand Palace and walk 5 minutes south to Wat Pho. Entry: THB 200 (~₹460). The Reclining Buddha is 46 metres long and covered in gold leaf — the scale of it inside the building is genuinely startling. The temple complex around it is one of the most beautiful in Bangkok. Budget 45 minutes to 1 hour. Wat Pho also has a famous traditional massage school on-site (THB 420 for 30 minutes, THB 780 for 1 hour) — worth booking here if you want a legitimate traditional Thai massage.

    Breakfast: There are local food stalls right outside the Wat Pho entrance on Maharaj Road — congee, fresh fruit, Thai tea. Eat here rather than the tourist cafes inside the complex.
  • Noon
    Chao Phraya Express Boat to Wat Arun

    Walk to Tha Tien pier (2 minutes from Wat Pho). Take the cross-river ferry to Wat Arun — THB 4 each way (~₹9), runs every few minutes. Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn) is best seen from across the river, but the closer you get the more extraordinary the surface detail becomes — the entire prang (tower) is covered in Chinese porcelain fragments and coloured glass. Entry: THB 100 (~₹230). Climb the steep central prang for river views. Budget 45 minutes.

  • 1:30pm
    Lunch on the river + Maharaj market

    Take the ferry back and walk along the Maharaj Road riverside — a covered market with local food stalls, cafes, and craft vendors in a renovated heritage building. Lunch here costs THB 80–150 (~₹185–345) for a full meal. Good vegetarian options — look for the yellow เจ flags. Eat slowly and rest — the morning was full on. If you're unsure what to order as a vegetarian traveller, our Jay (เจ) food survival guide covers exactly what to say and where to look.

  • Afternoon
    Optional: Chao Phraya Express Boat north

    Instead of taking Grab back, consider taking the orange-flag Chao Phraya Express Boat north from Tha Chang pier — THB 15 per boarding. You'll pass old merchant buildings, flower markets, and local life that no tourist bus shows you. Get off at Saphan Taksin pier and transfer to BTS.

  • Evening
    Silom area + rooftop or river dinner

    Silom is Bangkok's financial district and has a good mix of restaurants, bars, and night markets in the evening. If you want the rooftop bar experience, Sky Bar (Lebua) is nearby — budget THB 800–1,400 per person in minimum spend (2 cocktails). If you'd rather eat well for the same money: the restaurants along Silom Soi 20 and the MRT Silom area have excellent Thai, Indian, and international options. The Patpong Night Market (between Silom Soi 4 and 6) is worth a quick walk-through — mostly tourist goods but lively.

Budget Day 2: ₹3,500–5,500 Mid-range Day 2: ₹7,000–14,000

Day 3 Chatuchak Market · Jim Thompson House · National Museum · Sukhumvit Evening
  • 9am sharp
    Chatuchak Weekend Market — arrive at opening

    Saturday or Sunday only. Get here when it opens at 9am — by 11am it's a sweating, shuffling crowd and the best stalls have already sold their pieces. Sections worth targeting: Section 2–4 (vintage and second-hand clothing), Section 8 (Thai ceramics and crafts), Section 22 (antiques and collectibles), Section 7 (plants — genuinely spectacular). Budget 2–2.5 hours maximum. MRT to Chatuchak Park or BTS to Mo Chit. Wear the lightest clothes you own.

    Veg food at Chatuchak: Section 26 near the clock tower has food stalls including Jay options. Fresh fruit carts are everywhere — mango, pineapple, papaya, cut to order for THB 20–40.
  • 11:30am
    Leave Chatuchak + late breakfast/early lunch

    Exit before the heat becomes unbearable and find a cafe near the MRT for a sit-down meal. The Chatuchak area has good independent cafes near the park exit. Rest for 45 minutes — you'll need it for the afternoon.

  • 1pm
    Jim Thompson House

    An American silk merchant arrived in Bangkok after World War 2, revived the Thai silk industry, built a compound of six traditional Thai houses on a canal, and then vanished in the Malaysian jungle in 1967. The mystery was never solved. (The full story is in our Bangkok tips post — Tip 11.) The house is now a museum — THB 200 (~₹460), guided tours only, runs at fixed times. The architecture alone is worth it: traditional teak construction, elevated on stilts, surrounded by a canal garden. Budget 1 hour including the tour wait.

  • 2:30pm
    National Museum Bangkok (free on Sundays / THB 200 other days)

    Five minutes walk from Jim Thompson House. One of the best museums in Southeast Asia — a former 1874 palace complex with Thai art, Buddhist sculpture, royal regalia, and weapons spanning a millennium. Free on Sundays, THB 200 other days. Sunday mornings are uncrowded. If you're visiting on a weekday and the entry feels steep, the museum exterior and gardens are still free to walk through. Budget 1–1.5 hours inside.

  • Evening
    Sukhumvit night — street food, Terminal 21, or Asiatique

    Three options depending on your mood. Street food: The area around Asok BTS has excellent street food vendors from 6pm — noodles, grilled satay, mango sticky rice, fresh juice. Terminal 21 mall: Each floor is themed around a world city, the food court on the 5th floor (Pier 21) has some of the cheapest mall food in Bangkok — a full meal for THB 60–100 (~₹140–230). Asiatique the Riverfront: A riverside night market and mall hybrid — free shuttle boat from Saphan Taksin pier, open from 5pm, good for a casual evening walk and dinner.

Budget Day 3: ₹2,500–4,000 Mid-range Day 3: ₹5,000–9,000
If Day 3 Falls on a Weekday

Chatuchak is weekend-only. Swap Day 3 and Day 4 — do Ayutthaya on Day 3 and use the weekend day for Chatuchak. Alternatively, replace Chatuchak with the Damnoen Saduak Floating Market (1.5 hours from Bangkok by minivan, best before 10am) — touristy but visually spectacular.

Day 4 Ayutthaya Day Trip — Ancient Capital, Temple Ruins, UNESCO Heritage
  • 7am
    Early start — minivan from Victory Monument

    Minivans to Ayutthaya depart from Victory Monument (BTS Victory Monument station) from around 6am. Cost: THB 60–80 (~₹140–185) one way, 1.5 hours. No booking needed — just show up and join the queue. Alternatively, take the train from Hua Lamphong or Bang Sue station — THB 15–25 for a 3rd class seat, 1.5–2 hours, scenic and atmospheric. Leave Bangkok early to beat the heat — Ayutthaya at 2pm is brutal.

  • 9am
    Ayutthaya — the old capital

    Ayutthaya was Thailand's capital for 400 years and one of the largest cities on earth before it was sacked by the Burmese in 1767. What remains are temple ruins spread across an island in the river — headless Buddha statues, crumbling prangs, and the famous Wat Mahathat where a Buddha head is overgrown by tree roots. It is genuinely moving in a way that Bangkok temples, beautiful as they are, are not. Entry to individual temples: THB 50 each (~₹115). The main sites — Wat Mahathat, Wat Phra Si Sanphet, Wat Chaiwatthanaram — are within cycling distance of each other.

  • Morning
    Bicycle or tuk-tuk around the ruins

    Rent a bicycle near the minivan drop-off point (THB 50–80/day, ~₹115–185) and cycle between the temples at your own pace. This is the best way to do Ayutthaya — you stop when something interests you, you take the back lanes between ruins, and you cover more ground than any tuk-tuk tour. If the heat is getting to you, shared tuk-tuks offer island circuits for THB 200–300 per person. Budget 3–4 hours for the main ruins.

    Veg lunch in Ayutthaya: Roti Sai Mai stalls near the old palace area — a traditional Ayutthaya sweet made of cotton candy wrapped in thin roti. Not a full meal but a genuine local experience. Proper veg lunch: any Thai rice restaurant, ask for etsiz (no meat) or Jay.
  • 2pm
    Head back to Bangkok

    Don't push past 2pm in Ayutthaya — the afternoon heat is punishing and the main sites will have been fully visited. Return by minivan from the same stand (runs every 30–45 minutes throughout the day) or train. Back in Bangkok by 4–5pm. Rest at the hotel, clean up.

  • Evening
    Low-key Bangkok evening — Lumphini Park or local dinner

    After a full Ayutthaya day, keep the evening easy. Lumphini Park (Bangkok's version of Central Park, open until 8pm) is 5 minutes from Silom MRT — free, pleasant, and full of Bangkok residents doing evening exercise. Dinner nearby at a local restaurant — THB 100–200 for a full meal. Early night — Day 5 is departure but can still be used well.

Budget Day 4: ₹1,500–3,000 Mid-range Day 4: ₹3,500–6,000
Day 5 Slow Morning · Thai Massage · Shopping · Departure
  • Morning
    Thai massage + slow breakfast

    Book a traditional Thai massage at Wat Pho Massage School (THB 420 for 30 min, THB 780 for 1 hour) if you didn't go on Day 2 — or any reputable massage shop near your hotel. The ones inside shopping malls (Health Land, Let's Relax) are reliable chains with consistent quality. Avoid any shop with aggressive street touts. After the massage: a slow breakfast at a neighbourhood cafe. The area around Thong Lo BTS (Soi 55) has some of Bangkok's best independent breakfast cafes.

  • Late Morning
    Last shopping run

    Best places for last-minute Bangkok shopping: MBK Center near National Stadium BTS — electronics, clothing, souvenirs, all at fixed or negotiable prices, very Bangkok. Siam Paragon nearby for premium brands. Chatuchak Weekend Market if it's a Saturday or Sunday and you want one last pass. For Thai silk, Jim Thompson stores have the most reliable quality. For elephant pants, magnets, and tourist goods: anywhere in Khao San Road or Pratunam market.

  • Afternoon
    Check out, store luggage, last lunch

    Most hotels let you store luggage until your flight. Use the afternoon for one more Thai meal — the green curry, the khao man gai, the mango sticky rice that you said you'd have again and didn't. Then collect bags and Grab to the airport. Allow 2 hours before check-in for international flights, 2.5 hours if departing during peak traffic hours (4–7pm). Suvarnabhumi to Sukhumvit by Grab: THB 200–350, 30–60 minutes depending on traffic.

Budget Day 5: ₹2,000–4,000 Mid-range Day 5: ₹5,000–10,000
If You Have More Time

Don't pad Bangkok beyond Day 5. Add Chiang Mai (1-hour flight, THB 500–1,200 one way) for mountain air, ethical elephant sanctuaries, and far better vegetarian food. Or head to Koh Samui or Phuket for beaches. Bangkok is best at intensity — use extra days somewhere that's genuinely different.

Where to Stay in Bangkok

Best areas for Indian travellers: Sukhumvit (Asok/Nana BTS area) for first-timers — central, excellent transport, every price range. Silom for mid-range stays with easy access to both BTS and MRT. Old Town (Khao San Road area) for budget backpackers, though the metro connection is poor.

Getting Around Bangkok

Grab App

Primary transport for all journeys. Fixed price upfront, rated drivers, tracked routes. City centre rides: THB 50–120 (~₹115–275). Airport: THB 200–350. Download and set up before landing.

BTS Skytrain

Elevated rail covering Sukhumvit and Silom. THB 17–59 per journey. Fast, air-conditioned, avoids traffic. Best for east-west movement across the tourist corridor. Rabbit Card for stored value.

MRT Subway

Separate underground network covering Chinatown, old town areas, and the blue line circuit. Different ticket from BTS — you pay again when switching. THB 17–42 per journey.

Chao Phraya Boat

Orange-flag express boat: THB 15 per boarding, runs 6am–7pm. Best for the temple district. Cross-river ferry to Wat Arun: THB 4. Infinitely better than tourist boat packages (THB 200–400).
