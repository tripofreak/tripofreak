Our Verdict

Go — but choose your area wisely.

Bali scores 7.5/10 — not because it's mediocre, but because its weakest categories (transport and crowds) actively eat into your experience if you don't plan around them. The good news: if you know which area to stay in and when to go, Bali earns every rupee you spend on it.

Culture & Temples: 10/10

Photo: Unsplash

Bali is the only Hindu-majority island in Indonesia, and its culture is unlike anything you'll find anywhere else — including India. The Balinese practice a unique form of Hinduism blended with animism and Buddhism, and it shows in everything: the offerings left at temple gates every morning, the ceremonies that spill onto the street mid-afternoon, the gamelan music that drifts through rice fields at dusk.

As an Indian traveller, the familiarity is both comforting and fascinating. You'll recognise Ganesh, Saraswati, and Shiva — but rendered in an entirely different visual language, wrapped in black-and-white chequered cloth (poleng), surrounded by frangipani. Tanah Lot at sunset, Uluwatu with the kecak fire dance, and the sheer volume of temples in Ubud — Bali earns its perfect score without argument.

"The Balinese don't perform their culture for tourists. The ceremonies happen whether you're watching or not. That's what makes it feel real."

Budget ₹500–₹800 for entry to major temple complexes. Sarong and sash rental is usually included or costs ₹50. Don't skip the Tegallalang Rice Terraces — yes, they're crowded, but the Balinese irrigation system (subak) is a UNESCO World Heritage site for a reason.

Food & Eating Out: 9/10

Photo: Unsplash

Bali's food scene is exceptional — and that's across all budgets. Nasi goreng (fried rice), mie goreng (fried noodles), babi guling (suckling pig — skip if vegetarian, a must-try if not), and sate lilit are the local staples. At a warung (local eatery), a full meal with a drink costs ₹150–₹300. The same dish at a Seminyak café with mood lighting runs ₹600–₹900 — your call.

The smoothie bowl trend started in Bali, and it's still better here than anywhere else. Canggu is the epicentre — expect ₹300–₹500 for a bowl that's actually filling. For Indians: vegetarian options are plentiful — Hindu influence means most warungs have separate veggie menus, often featuring jackfruit curries and tempeh dishes that rival anything back home.

The one area Bali loses a point: alcohol is expensive relative to the rest of Southeast Asia. A Bintang beer at a beach club runs ₹400–₹700. If you're used to Bangkok's ₹150 Chang, adjust expectations.

Accommodation: 9/10

Photo: Unsplash

This is where Bali genuinely surprises. The value-to-luxury ratio for accommodation is almost unmatched in Asia. A private pool villa — your own plunge pool, open-air bathroom, outdoor daybed — costs ₹4,000–₹8,000 per night in Ubud or Canggu. In Seminyak or Nusa Dua, expect ₹8,000–₹18,000 for the same quality. That's a five-star experience at a three-star price.

Budget travellers aren't left out. Clean, well-designed hostels and guesthouses in Kuta start at ₹800–₹1,500 per night. Mid-range hotel rooms with pools run ₹2,500–₹4,000. The design standards across the board are unusually high — Bali has a strong tradition of beautiful architecture, and even budget properties tend to be thoughtfully made.

Area matters more than budget here. Canggu is the best all-rounder for 2026 — good food, surf beach, less chaotic than Kuta, more affordable than Seminyak. Ubud is for culture and rice terrace views. Nusa Dua is for pure resort luxury. Avoid Kuta unless you're specifically here for the party scene — it's noisy, crowded, and the beach is mediocre.

"Book the villa with a private pool. You think it's a splurge until you're floating in it at 7am, looking at rice terraces, for ₹5,500 a night."

Getting Around: 5/10 — The Real Problem

There is no public transport in Bali worth mentioning. Your options are: rent a scooter (₹300–₹500/day), hire a private driver (₹2,500–₹4,000/day — worth it for temple hopping), or use Grab (works in most areas, rates are fair). The problem is traffic — specifically in Kuta, Seminyak, and Ubud. A 5km journey can take 45 minutes at peak hours. Plan your days to move early (before 9am) or late (after 7pm).

Compare this to Krabi, where everything is compact and accessible — Bali requires more effort to get around, and that effort has a real cost in time and patience. Traffic is the single biggest drain on the Bali experience, and it only gets worse each year.

6 Things to Know Before You Go
01

Go in shoulder season. April–May or September–October. Dry weather, 30% fewer crowds, same beaches.

02

Stay in Canggu. Best balance of food, surf, culture, and sanity. Skip Kuta unless you're here specifically for the party.

03

Indonesia's drug laws are zero-tolerance. Possession can mean life imprisonment. Not a single exception, ever.

04

Hire a driver for temple day. Tanah Lot → Uluwatu → Tegallalang in one day costs ₹3,000–₹4,000. Worth every rupee.

05

Dress respectfully at temples. Sarong and covered shoulders are mandatory. Most temples rent sarongs at the gate for ₹50.

06

Book the pool villa. Private villa with plunge pool for ₹5,000–₹7,000/night. It's the Bali experience. Do it at least once.
