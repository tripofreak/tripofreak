Singapore Honest Scorecard 2026: Is It Worth It for Indians?

September 2026 · 10 min read · Rated across 8 categories
Share WhatsApp X

Singapore is India's most popular short-haul international destination for a reason — it's close, it's clean, it's safe, and it has great food. It's also the most expensive country in Southeast Asia by a wide margin. So before you book, here's an honest category-by-category score from a traveller's perspective, not a tourism board's.

Food: 9/10 — The Hawker Centre Alone Is Worth the Trip

Singapore's hawker centres are UNESCO-listed — and rightfully so. Maxwell Food Centre, Lau Pa Sat, Old Airport Road — these are outdoor food courts where Michelin-quality dishes cost ₹300–600. Hainanese chicken rice (the national dish), char kway teow, laksa, roti prata, fish head curry — all done to exceptional standard by hawkers who've been perfecting one dish for decades.

For Indian travellers specifically, Singapore is uniquely comfortable. Little India (Serangoon Road) has South Indian restaurants, North Indian dhabas, Tamil tea shops, and vegetarian-only places that rival anything in Chennai or Delhi. You will never struggle to find good vegetarian food here — unlike almost every other Southeast Asian destination.

The downside: restaurant dining (as opposed to hawker stalls) is expensive. A sit-down dinner for two with drinks at a mid-range restaurant in Clarke Quay: ₹4,000–7,000. Fine dining: ₹8,000–15,000 per person. Eat at hawkers for lunch and dinner, treat yourself to one nice restaurant — that's the smart approach.

Value for Money: 5/10 — The Most Expensive Country in Southeast Asia

This is where Singapore gets an honest low score — and where most Indian travel guides soften the truth. Singapore is significantly more expensive than anywhere else in the region. Compare daily on-ground costs:

  • Singapore: ₹6,000–10,000/day (excluding hotel)
  • Bali: ₹2,500–4,000/day
  • Vietnam: ₹1,500–3,000/day
  • Bangkok: ₹2,000–3,500/day

A mid-range hotel in Singapore costs ₹5,000–8,000/night. The same money gets you a genuinely good resort in Bali or a 4-star in Vietnam. Attraction costs add up — Universal Studios is ₹5,500/person, Gardens by the Bay is free but the domes cost ₹1,200 each. The Singapore cable car: ₹1,400. Night Safari: ₹3,500.

None of this means Singapore isn't worth it. The quality, safety, and experience justify the cost. But go in with eyes open — a 5-day Singapore trip costs roughly the same as a 10-day Vietnam trip.

Transport: 10/10 — The Best Metro in Asia

Singapore's MRT (Mass Rapid Transit) is the gold standard of urban transport. Every major attraction, every neighbourhood, every airport terminal is connected. Trains run every 2–5 minutes. Air-conditioned. Never delayed. Signs in English. Tap your Visa/Mastercard contactless or use Apple Pay/Google Pay — no special tourist card needed. A single ride: ₹100–200 depending on distance.

Grab is available for when you need point-to-point, but honestly the MRT eliminates the need for taxis 90% of the time. The airport (Changi) is 30 minutes from the city centre by MRT — ₹150. No airport taxi scam is even possible here.

Safety: 10/10 — The Safest City in Asia

Singapore has essentially no street crime. The scams that plague Bangkok, Bali, and even Hanoi simply don't exist here. No fake gem stores, no tuktuk drivers taking you to commission shops, no fake monks, no card games in alleys. This is partly culture, partly the extremely strict legal system (fines for littering, jaywalking, eating on the MRT — they're enforced), and partly the CCTV coverage that is genuinely comprehensive.

Solo women travellers consistently rate Singapore as the most comfortable city in Asia. You can walk anywhere at any hour. Leave your laptop at a café table while you queue for coffee — it'll be there when you return. This level of ease has real value, especially for first-time international travellers from India.

The Verdict: Who Should Go

Go if: You want a worry-free, premium Southeast Asia trip. You're travelling with family and kids. It's your first international trip. You value safety and efficiency above all. You want excellent food without the chaos of Bangkok or Vietnam.

Skip if: Budget is tight — there are better value destinations (Vietnam scores 7.9/10 on our scorecard at half the cost). You want exotic landscapes, beaches, or temples. You want more than 5 days of genuine exploration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Singapore worth visiting in 2026 for Indians?+
Yes — especially for families, first-time international travellers, and anyone who values safety and efficiency. It's expensive but the quality is consistent. Go in knowing it costs significantly more than Bali or Vietnam, and you'll have a great trip.
How much does a Singapore trip cost from India?+
Budget: ₹55,000–75,000 per person for 5 days including return flights. Mid-range: ₹90,000–1,30,000. Flights from Indian metros: ₹18,000–30,000 return. On-ground costs: ₹6,000–10,000/day excluding accommodation. Hawker food keeps food costs manageable — ₹500–800/day eating at hawker centres.
Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?+
Yes — Indian passport holders need an e-visa. Apply online at the ICA Singapore website. Takes 3–5 working days, costs approximately SGD 30 (₹1,900). Apply at least 2 weeks before travel. Approval rates are high for Indian applicants with a clean travel history.
Is Singapore safe for Indian tourists?+
Singapore is one of the safest cities in the world. There is essentially no street crime, no scam culture, and no tourist traps. Solo women travellers rate it the highest of any Asian destination. The main "danger" is accidentally breaking a rule — fines for littering and jaywalking are real and enforced.
How many days is enough for Singapore?+
4–5 days covers the highlights comfortably: Marina Bay, Gardens by the Bay, Sentosa/Universal Studios, Little India, Chinatown, Night Safari, and a day trip to the Southern Ridges or Pulau Ubin. 3 days is tight but doable if you focus. 6+ days and you'll start to feel like you've exhausted it.
