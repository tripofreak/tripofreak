Vietnam: Brilliant Value, Real Trade-offs

One of Southeast Asia's best destinations for Indian travellers — visa-free, dirt cheap, and with food and culture that will genuinely surprise you. A few rough edges keep it from perfection, but nothing that should stop you from going.

UNESCO World Heritage sites, French colonial architecture, ancient Cham temples, 54 ethnic groups. Genuinely layered history that rewards curiosity.

Food

Arguably the best street food in Asia. Pho, banh mi, bun cha, cao lau, fresh spring rolls. Every city has its own specialties. The only caveat: not very spicy, and vegetarians need to plan.

Culture & History: 9/10 — Deeper Than You Expect

Vietnam's history is the first thing that hits you — and it hits hard. This is a country that fought and won against the Mongols, the Chinese, the French, the Americans, and the Khmer. Walking through Hanoi's Old Quarter, you're walking through 1,000 years of commerce. Each of the 36 original streets was named for what was sold there — silk, paper, tin, bamboo.

Hoi An is the preserved merchant port that trade built, a UNESCO town where Japanese merchants, Chinese traders, and Vietnamese craftspeople all left their mark on the architecture. It's one of the most genuinely beautiful old towns in Asia — not a reconstruction, the real thing.

If you're comparing depth of culture across destinations, read our Japan Honest Scorecard — Japan scores higher on culture at 10/10, but Vietnam offers a very different, more accessible kind of immersion.

For Indian travellers specifically: There's a Cham heritage trail through central Vietnam (My Son Sanctuary, Po Nagar Towers) that traces an ancient Hindu kingdom. If you grew up with temple architecture and Sanskrit influences, My Son will feel unexpectedly familiar.

The War Remnants Museum in HCMC and the Cu Chi Tunnels are not light experiences. But they're important ones. Vietnam's relationship with its 20th century is complex, raw, and handled with more nuance than you might expect.

Food: 9/10 — You Will Think About This Food for Years

Vietnamese food is the most underrated cuisine in the world. It's built on fresh herbs, clear broths, fermented balance, and contrast — hot and cold, crispy and soft, sour and savoury. A bowl of pho is a 12-hour process. A banh mi from a street cart will cost you ₹30 and be better than anything from an expensive restaurant back home.

Every region has its own specialties. Hanoi does bun cha (grilled pork and noodles in broth — what Anthony Bourdain ate with Obama). Hue does the spiciest food in Vietnam — bun bo Hue will wake you up. Hoi An has cao lau, white rose dumplings, and banh mi that's internationally famous. HCMC has banh xeo (sizzling crepes) and the best pho in the south.

Vegetarian warning: Fish sauce (nuoc mam) is the base of Vietnamese cooking and goes into almost everything. Pure vegetarians will struggle outside of dedicated vegetarian restaurants (which do exist in all major cities). Buddhist Vietnamese cuisine (com chay) is excellent — seek it out.

The bia hoi culture deserves its own mention. Fresh beer brewed daily, served on tiny plastic stools at street corners, for ₹30-50 a glass. Hanoi's Bia Hoi corner at the intersection of Ta Hien and Luong Ngoc Quyen is one of the great people-watching spots in Asia.

On the food front, Vietnam competes seriously with Thailand. If you've been to Bangkok and loved the street food scene, Vietnam will surprise you with how different — and arguably better — the flavours are. See how we rated Bangkok on our Bangkok Honest Scorecard.

Safety: 8/10 — Mostly Excellent, Stay Aware in HCMC

Vietnam is one of the safer countries in Southeast Asia. The most common danger is traffic — Vietnamese cities operate on a logic all their own, with motorbikes flowing around you like water. Cross the road slowly and steadily, never stop suddenly, and let the traffic read you.

Bag snatching from moving motorbikes is a genuine problem in Ho Chi Minh City — don't walk with your phone out or bag hanging loosely. Tourist scams (inflated tuk-tuk fares, fake tour operators, gem scams) exist in tourist areas. Book through reputable platforms and you'll be fine.

Transport: 7/10 — Cities Are Easy, Distances Are Deceptive

Vietnam is long and thin — 1,650 km from north to south. Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City is the distance from Delhi to Chennai. The Reunification Express train covers it in 33 hours. Most smart travellers mix budget airlines (VietJet, Bamboo Airways) for long legs with local transport in each city.

Grab is the default for city transport and works perfectly in Hanoi, HCMC, Da Nang, and Hoi An. Motorbike taxis (Grab Bike) are cheap and fast. Local buses exist but are hard to navigate without Vietnamese. For day trips and tours, reputable operators are genuinely better value than trying to DIY everything.

Accommodation: 8/10 — Outstanding Value Across All Budgets

Vietnam punches well above its weight on accommodation quality per rupee spent. Hostels with private rooms and ensuite bathrooms run ₹700-1,200/night. Mid-range hotels in Hoi An — often beautifully designed Vietnamese town houses with small pools — cost ₹2,500-4,500/night. Luxury properties that would cost ₹25,000+ in Thailand are available for ₹8,000-12,000/night.

Hoi An in particular has some of the best boutique accommodation value in all of Asia. Book early for peak season (Dec-Feb), as the best properties fill up fast. For a comparison, Bali offers similar boutique value — see our Bali Honest Scorecard to decide which fits your trip better.

Crowd Factor: 6/10 — The Popular Spots Are Very Popular

Vietnam's biggest tourist sites are genuinely overrun. Halong Bay at peak season has hundreds of cruise boats anchored in the same postcard bays. Hoi An Ancient Town in December is shoulder-to-shoulder. Sapa's rice terraces are trekked by thousands every week.

This doesn't mean skip them — they're iconic for good reason. But set your expectations correctly. If you want Halong Bay without the crowd, look at Lan Ha Bay (next door, less traffic) or Bai Tu Long Bay (further from the tourist boats). For Sapa, go offseason or trek to less-visited villages.

Best time from India: November to April. South Vietnam (HCMC, Mekong Delta) is dry Dec-April. Central Vietnam (Hoi An, Hue, Da Nang) is best Feb-April. North Vietnam (Hanoi, Halong Bay, Sapa) is best Oct-Dec and March-May. Avoid August-September for central Vietnam — typhoon season.

Value for Money: 9/10 — The Best Bang-per-Rupee in Asia

Vietnam is consistently one of the cheapest quality destinations in the world. Street food meals: ₹60-150. Sit-down restaurant with beer: ₹300-600 per person. Budget guesthouse: ₹700-1,200/night. Comfortable mid-range hotel: ₹2,500-4,500/night. Half-day cooking class: ₹1,500-2,500.

A 10-day trip to Vietnam including direct flights from Delhi or Mumbai (around ₹20,000-35,000 return), accommodation, food, internal transport, and activities can be done comfortably for ₹70,000-1,10,000 per person. That's extraordinary value for the experience you get.

The Honest Verdict

Vietnam is one of those destinations that overshoots expectations. The food is world-class, the history is genuinely fascinating, and it's visa-free for Indians — which removes one of the biggest friction points for a spontaneous trip.

The crowd problem at marquee sites is real, and transport logistics require a bit more planning than Thailand or Bali. But these are manageable inconveniences, not dealbreakers. If you've been sitting on the fence about Vietnam, this is the push: go.

The only Indians who come back disappointed are those who expected Thai food, Thai beach density, or Thai infrastructure. Vietnam is its own country, with its own logic. Embrace that and you'll love it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Vietnam? +
Vietnam offers visa-free entry to Indian passport holders for up to 45 days as of 2024. You can also get an e-visa easily online for longer stays or if you need a multi-entry option. This is a huge plus over destinations like Japan or the US.
What is the best time to visit Vietnam from India? +
November to April is the best overall window. South Vietnam (HCMC, Mekong) is dry December-April. Central Vietnam (Hoi An, Hue) is best February-April. North Vietnam (Hanoi, Halong Bay) is best October-December and March-April. Avoid August-September for central Vietnam due to typhoon risk.
Is Vietnam safe for Indian tourists? +
Yes, Vietnam is generally very safe. The main risks are petty theft (motorbike bag snatching in HCMC) and tourist scams around inflated prices and fake tours. Violent crime against tourists is rare. Stay aware in crowded tourist areas and book through reputable operators.
How much does a Vietnam trip cost from India? +
Budget travellers can manage on ₹3,000-4,000/day. A comfortable mid-range trip runs ₹6,000-9,000/day including accommodation, food, transport, and activities. A 10-day trip including return flights from India costs roughly ₹70,000-1,20,000 per person — exceptional value.
Is Vietnamese food good for Indian vegetarians? +
Vietnamese food is excellent but challenging for strict vegetarians. Fish sauce (nuoc mam) is a base ingredient in most dishes. Cities have dedicated vegetarian restaurants (look for "com chay" — Buddhist Vietnamese cuisine), and these are genuinely good. Plan ahead, especially in smaller towns.
How many days do you need in Vietnam? +
Minimum 10 days to cover the key highlights (Hanoi + Halong Bay + Hoi An + HCMC). Two weeks is more comfortable. Three weeks lets you add Hue, Sapa, the Mekong Delta, or Phu Quoc without rushing.
