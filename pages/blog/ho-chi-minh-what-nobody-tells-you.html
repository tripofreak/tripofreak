Ho Chi Minh City: What Nobody Tells You Before You Go

September 2026 · 12 min read · 12 things guides skip
Share WhatsApp X

Ho Chi Minh City (still called Saigon by almost everyone who lives here) is the kind of place that overwhelms you in the first hour and gets under your skin by Day 3. It's louder than Hanoi, faster than Bangkok, and more chaotic than anything most first-time visitors are prepared for. Every guide tells you about the War Remnants Museum and the Cu Chi Tunnels. Nobody tells you the things that will actually determine whether your trip is good or frustrating.

Here are 12 of them. Read our Vietnam Honest Scorecard if you're still deciding whether to go.

1
The streets

HCMC's traffic is not like Hanoi's traffic — it's a different beast entirely

Hanoi has motorbike chaos. HCMC has motorbike chaos plus cars, buses, trucks, and construction at all hours. Crossing the road in Hanoi has a rhythm — step in slowly, let the flow part around you. HCMC has the same philosophy but the speeds are higher and the gaps are narrower. Your first road crossing will be terrifying. By Day 2 you'll have it figured out. The trick: don't stop, don't run. Move at a slow, predictable pace and the traffic reads you.

2
Transport

Use Grab for everything. Literally everything.

HCMC has a well-documented taxi scam problem — meters that spin fast, drivers who "misunderstand" the destination, and fares that end up 3x what they should be. Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) eliminates all of this. Fixed prices, GPS tracking, the driver can't deviate. Grab Car: ₹150–300 for most city rides. Grab Bike (motorbike taxi): ₹60–120. Grab Bike is often faster in traffic and half the price. Set it up before you land — you'll need it from the airport on Day 1.

3
Districts

District 1 is for tourists. District 3 is where you actually want to eat.

District 1 is convenient and central but restaurants here mark up 40–80% for the location. District 3, a 10-minute Grab away, has the same quality food at local prices. Ben Thanh Market is the tourist trap epicentre — the food stalls inside are overpriced and aggressively marketed. Instead, walk one block to the streets behind it: Vo Van Tan, Pasteur, Le Van Sy in District 3 for proper com tam (broken rice), banh mi, and pho at ₹80–150 a meal. District 4 (south of D1, across the river) is HCMC's seafood district — cheap, excellent, very local.

4
Food

Com tam (broken rice) is the signature dish — not pho

Pho is from the north. In HCMC, the local obsession is com tam — broken rice grains (a byproduct of milling) served with grilled pork chop (suon nuong), shredded pork skin, fried egg, pickled vegetables, and nuoc cham dipping sauce. ₹80–150 at a street stall, ₹200–300 at a sit-down place. You'll see com tam stalls everywhere, usually with plastic stools on the pavement. Sit down. Order the suon nuong. This is the dish HCMC is actually famous for — most guides bury it under generic "Vietnamese food" headings.

5
History

The War Remnants Museum is not easy viewing — but it's essential

Every travel guide says "go to the War Remnants Museum." What they don't say: this is one of the most confronting museum experiences you will have anywhere in the world. The third floor has graphic photographs from the American war. Children sometimes cry. Adults sometimes cry. You will exit different from how you entered. Go anyway — it gives HCMC a weight that makes the rest of the city make more sense. Entry is ₹40. Allow 2–3 hours. Go in the morning when it's less crowded.

6
Day trip

Cu Chi Tunnels: book a small group tour, not the big bus

Cu Chi Tunnels (70 km from HCMC, 1.5–2 hrs each way) are one of the most remarkable things you can see in Vietnam — 250 km of underground tunnels used during the war. The problem: most tours pack 40–50 people onto a bus, give you 90 minutes at the site, and spend more time at a lacquerware shop than the tunnels. Book a small group tour (max 8–10 people, ₹800–1,200 per person) or hire a private driver (₹2,000–3,000 return). Spend at least 2.5 hours at the site. You can actually crawl through the tunnels — do it, even if it's claustrophobic.

7
Safety

Bag snatching is real — always walk with your bag on the building side

Drive-by bag snatching from motorbikes is HCMC's most common crime against tourists. The method: you're walking on the pavement with your bag on the road side, a motorbike comes close, grabs the strap, and accelerates. Prevention is simple: keep your bag on the wall/building side, not the road side. Don't walk while looking at your phone near traffic. Don't drape your phone over café railings near the street. This isn't paranoia — it happens regularly, especially in District 1. Once you know to keep the bag on the inside, the risk drops dramatically.

8
Nightlife

Bui Vien is the backpacker strip — fun for one night, grating after that

Bui Vien Walking Street is HCMC's famous party district: neon lights, ₹30 beers, cover bands, tourist bars packed wall-to-wall. It's genuinely chaotic and worth one night. But for a second night out, go elsewhere — Pasteur Street Brewing Company (craft beer, District 1), Chill Skybar on the 26th floor of AB Tower (rooftop views, ₹400–600 cocktails), or the quieter bars along Bui Thi Xuan in District 1. Locals don't go to Bui Vien. District 3's bar scene is more mixed and less tourist-saturated.

9
Weather

It's 30–35°C year-round, and the wet season downpours are sudden and total

Unlike Hanoi, HCMC has no real cool season — it sits just north of the equator and stays hot and humid regardless of month. The wet season (May–November) brings afternoon downpours that last 30–60 minutes and flood streets within minutes. They're intense but pass quickly. Always carry a thin packable rain jacket or buy a cheap Vietnamese rain poncho (₹40 from any street vendor). Don't bother with an umbrella — the wind makes them useless. The dry season (December–April) is the "better" time but it's still 32°C.

10
Budget

HCMC is cheap — but District 1 restaurants will charge you Western prices if you let them

A proper local meal in HCMC costs ₹80–200. A beer at a street stall: ₹40–60. A Grab across the city: ₹150. This is genuinely one of the cheapest major cities in Asia. But tourist-facing restaurants in District 1 — especially those with English menus displayed outside and staff who wave you in from the door — charge ₹500–1,200 for the same food. The tell: if the menu has photos and prices in US dollars first, it's a tourist restaurant. Walk one block back from the main road. Sit where locals sit. The quality is always better and the price is always lower.

11
Context

HCMC and Hanoi are like two different countries — don't expect the same vibe

Travellers who visit Hanoi first and then HCMC are often shocked. Hanoi is older, more atmospheric, cooler in temperature and pace — French colonial architecture, tree-lined lakes, a certain melancholy. HCMC is commerce, energy, construction, and ambition. It looks more like a modern Asian megalopolis. The food is different, the people are considered more business-oriented and direct, and the city is expanding rapidly. Neither is better — they're just completely different. If you loved Hanoi's quiet lanes and old town feel, HCMC will feel jarring. Give it 48 hours before you judge. 12
Timing

3 days minimum. 5 days if you want to add the Mekong Delta.

Most visitors rush HCMC in 2 days and feel like they missed it. The Mekong Delta (2–3 hours south) is one of Vietnam's most underrated experiences — rice paddies, floating markets, boat trips through canal networks, and fruit orchards. It deserves a full day trip minimum, ideally a 2-day overnight. If you're doing our Vietnam 10-day itinerary, 3 days in HCMC is the baseline. If you can extend to 5, add the Mekong. It's the one thing that changes how you understand southern Vietnam.

The Honest Verdict

HCMC doesn't seduce you the way Hoi An does or impress you the way Halong Bay does. It overwhelms you, frustrates you, then slowly reveals itself as one of the most alive cities in Asia. The food is excellent, the history is heavy, the energy is relentless. Go in knowing that — and you'll leave loving it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ho Chi Minh City safe for tourists?+
Generally yes — violent crime against tourists is rare. The main risks are bag snatching by motorbike and taxi scams. Use Grab for all rides and keep your bag on the building side of the pavement, not the road side. That eliminates 90% of the risk.
How many days do you need in Ho Chi Minh City?+
3 days minimum for the city highlights (Cu Chi Tunnels, War Remnants Museum, food, colonial architecture). 5 days if you want to add a Mekong Delta day trip or overnight. Don't try to do it in 2 days — you'll feel like you missed it.
What is the best district to stay in Ho Chi Minh City?+
District 1 for first-timers — it's central, walkable, and has the most tourist infrastructure. District 3 is better for a more local experience with better food at lower prices. Both are good choices depending on your priority.
Is Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi better?+
They're completely different. Hanoi is atmospheric, historic, and slower-paced — better for culture and Halong Bay access. HCMC is faster, more commercial, and more intense — better for food variety, nightlife, and Cu Chi/Mekong. Most travellers prefer Hanoi for the vibe but find HCMC more energising.
What should I avoid in Ho Chi Minh City?+
Avoid unlicensed taxis (use Grab), Ben Thanh Market for shopping (tourist prices), carrying your bag on the road side of the pavement, and restaurants that aggressively wave you in from the doorway. Also avoid rushing — give HCMC at least 3 days before you judge it.
