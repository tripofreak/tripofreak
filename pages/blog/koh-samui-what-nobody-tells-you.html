6 Things Nobody Tells You
About Koh Samui

8 min read September 2026 All traveller types

Photo: Unsplash

Every photo of Koh Samui looks the same. A coconut palm leaning over glassy water. A longtail boat going somewhere perfect. A plate of pad thai that catches the light just right. The island has been in the tourism business since the 1970s and it knows exactly how it wants to look. What it doesn't tell you — what no resort website, no influencer reel, and no "top 10 things to do" list will say out loud — is considerably more useful than any of that.

We went in April. We came back with opinions. Here they are.

1. Koh Samui Has Two Monsoon Seasons — and Most Travellers Book the Wrong Window

Photo: Unsplash

Here is the thing that will ruin your planning if you don't know it: Koh Samui sits in the Gulf of Thailand, on the opposite side of the peninsula from Phuket and Krabi. When the southwest monsoon hits the Andaman Coast from May to October, Koh Samui is mostly fine. When the northeast monsoon arrives from October to December, Koh Samui gets genuinely hammered — while the rest of Thailand is enjoying perfect weather.

October and November are the worst months. There have been years where flights were cancelled for days. Flooding around Chaweng is not unusual. If a travel agent, a budget flight deal, or a long weekend lines up for late October, do not go to Koh Samui. Go to Krabi instead. The best window for Koh Samui is February to April — dry, breezy, calm sea, hotels running deals because it's shoulder season compared to December and January.

2. Chaweng Beach Is Not the Whole Island — and It's the Part You Should Skip

Photo: Unsplash

Chaweng is Koh Samui's main strip — 7km of beach flanked by hotels, bars, massage parlours, and souvenir shops. It is also the part of the island most heavily photographed and most aggressively marketed. The beach itself is fine: wide, sandy, palm-backed. But it is never quiet. Not at 6am. Not in shoulder season. The water in front of the central stretch can be murky after rain. The road behind it is gridlocked by noon.

The beaches that look like the brochure photos are elsewhere. Silver Beach (also called Crystal Bay) in the east is small, sheltered, and genuinely beautiful. Lipa Noi on the west coast has a long, calm beach with almost no development — it faces a bay, not open ocean, so the water is flat even when the east side gets choppy. Ban Tai in the north is quiet and loved by locals. These are thirty to forty minutes from Chaweng. Most people staying at Chaweng never make it. That is fine. It means fewer people at Silver Beach.

The actual rule: The more Instagram-famous a beach on Koh Samui is, the less it looks like its photos in real life. Work backwards from the map. The beaches with no famous name are almost always the better ones.

3. Getting Around Without a Scooter Is Slow and Expensive

Photo: Unsplash

Koh Samui has no public bus system worth discussing. There are songthaews — red pickup trucks that operate loosely like shared taxis on fixed routes — but they are erratic, often don't run past 6pm, and require you to know exactly where you're going and how to flag one down. Grab works in Samui but is frequently surge-priced, especially in Chaweng and especially at night. A basic trip from Chaweng to the Fisherman's Village market in Bophut costs ₹400–600 by Grab. A scooter rental costs ₹700–900 per day. The math makes itself.

If you are comfortable on a scooter, rent one on day one and don't think about it again. The ring road around the island is mostly smooth and the traffic, while real, is not Bangkok. If you are not comfortable on a scooter, budget ₹2,500–3,500 per day for Grab rides and accept that your movement will be limited. There is no in-between solution. Koh Samui is 228 square kilometres. The things worth doing are not all in one place.

4. The Food Price Gap Is Wider Than You Expect

Photo: Unsplash

A plate of pad thai at a local shophouse on Koh Samui costs ₹120–160. The same dish at a beachfront restaurant in Chaweng costs ₹600–900. Both are pad thai. Both are fine. The difference is entirely about who is standing between you and the sea when you eat it. If you eat every meal inside the resort zone, you will spend three to four times as much as someone who walks one street back from the beach. The Fisherman's Village night market in Bophut — open Friday evenings — is the best cheap food on the island and also happens to be the most pleasant atmosphere.

One thing that genuinely surprises Indian travellers: seafood is fantastic and affordable outside the tourist belt. A fresh whole grilled fish at a local place runs ₹350–500. Crab, prawn, squid — all available, all good, all priced for locals rather than holidaymakers. The strategy is simple: eat your first meal somewhere expensive to get your bearings, then find the street you'll actually eat at for the rest of the trip. Ask your guesthouse, not TripAdvisor. 5. The Full Moon Party Is Not Here — And Koh Samui's Nightlife Is Not What It Was

Photo: Unsplash

The Full Moon Party is on Koh Phangan, not Koh Samui. They are two different islands. Koh Phangan is a 30-minute ferry ride from Koh Samui and the party happens on Had Rin beach once a month on the full moon. This is worth stating plainly because a significant number of first-time visitors arrive on Koh Samui expecting the party island experience and find that Chaweng's nightlife — while active — is more loud bars and bucket cocktails than anything resembling a beach rave.

Chaweng is still fun at night if that's what you're after. But it's Pattaya-adjacent in character, not the Ibiza-lite fantasy that the marketing implies. The better evening on Koh Samui is quieter: the Fisherman's Village market, sunset at a west-coast beach, dinner at a good seafood spot, and an early night because you're going to rent a scooter tomorrow and drive to Silver Beach before anyone else gets there. 6. The Luxury Resorts Are Half the Price If You Know When to Book

Photo: Unsplash

Koh Samui is home to some of the best-value luxury in Asia. The Four Seasons, Anantara, Six Senses, Vana Belle — these are genuine five-star properties with private pool villas, multi-course dinners, and the kind of design that makes you feel like your life has gone very right. In peak season (December–January), private pool villas can cost ₹35,000–60,000 per night. In March and April — the best weather window — the same rooms often drop to ₹18,000–28,000. That is still a significant spend, but the value shift is real and the experience is better because the resort is quieter.

The lesson is timing. Koh Samui is not a budget island — the flight from India, the resort rates, and the transfer costs make it naturally more expensive than Bali or a comparable Thai beach. But if you are going to go, February to April is the window where the weather, the crowds, and the prices all line up. Book directly with the hotel when possible (better rates, better flexibility). What we actually learned

  • Koh Samui's monsoon is October–December, not May–October like the rest of Thailand. Book February–April.
  • Chaweng beach is fine but overcrowded. Silver Beach, Lipa Noi, and Ban Tai are the real ones.
  • No public transport worth relying on. Rent a scooter or budget heavily for Grab.
  • Food price gap is 3–4x between tourist and local zones. Walk one street back every time.
  • The Full Moon Party is on Koh Phangan, 30 minutes by ferry. Not Koh Samui.
  • Luxury resorts in shoulder season (Feb–Apr) drop to near half the peak price with better weather.
6 things to do differently
TIP 01

Book Feb–Apr. Best weather, lowest crowds, best resort rates. Avoid October–November entirely.

TIP 02

Stay west or north. Lipa Noi or Bophut for a quieter, cheaper, more local experience than Chaweng.

TIP 03

Rent the scooter. Day one. ₹700–900/day. The ring road is manageable and the island is too spread out without one.

TIP 04

Eat at the Fisherman's Village. Friday night market in Bophut. Best food, best atmosphere, cheapest prices on the island.

TIP 05

Fill the TDAC before you fly. Thailand Digital Arrival Card online, 72 hours before departure. Missing it causes gate issues.

TIP 06

Get to Silver Beach early. Before 9am it looks like the brochure. By 11am, so does the car park next to it.

Budget roughly ₹80,000–₹1,20,000 per person for 6–7 nights including flights from Delhi or Mumbai (via Bangkok). Mid-range hotel, scooter rental, meals, and island-hopping day trips are affordable once you're there — ₹2,000–₹3,500 per day is realistic outside the resort zone.

As of 2026, Indian passport holders get a 60-day visa-free entry into Thailand. No visa on arrival paperwork, no fee. You will need to fill a Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) online before you fly — do it at least 72 hours before departure to avoid issues at the gate.
